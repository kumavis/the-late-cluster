# 1. Premise and point of view

## What the show is

*The Late Cluster with Larry Nx* is a late-night talk show about one corner of Bluesky that calls
itself the simcluster: tech, AI and builder posters who make things for each other in public. Every
voice, song, laugh and clap is synthesized by CHIMERA, a physical-model vocal synth. The performers
are glowing vector heads on puppet bodies in a drawn studio. Guests are puppets playing the posting
personas of real accounts, and the screen says so.

It is weekly and runs about eight minutes. It is researched, written and staged by models from what
the community posted since the last episode, and approved by a person before it airs.

## The first rule

"Only include it if it's interesting", with its test, is Article 1 of the
[constitution](00-constitution.md). It overrules everything in this chapter and every other chapter
of the DNA, the standing segments included.

## Who it is for

In priority order:

1. **The cluster itself.** The show is posted to Bluesky. Its first viewers are the people it is
   about, and some of them will see their own posts and projects on screen. The episode is a gift to
   them: it should make them feel seen, accurately, and a little teased.
2. **Someone who missed the week,** or who is next door to the cluster. Every joke has to parse for
   them from what is said and shown, even if an insider gets a second layer.
3. **The archive.** Episodes stay online. A joke should still make sense to someone watching a year
   later, which is one more reason to put the setup on screen and say it aloud instead of assuming
   the viewer read the timeline.

## Point of view

- **The show is about a community, not about itself.** Its subject every week is what people in the
  cluster made, said and did since the last episode. Its own past, its reception and its habits are
  not its subject ([do not repeat the show](03-episode-shape.md#do-not-repeat-the-show)).
- **It is inside the thing it is about.** It is made with the same kind of tools, by the same kind of
  people, and posted in the same place. It never explains the cluster to outsiders as a curiosity
  and never sneers at it. When it jokes about the cluster it is joking about itself.
- **Making something for someone is how these people show affection.** A thing somebody made and
  showed is treated as a gift to the scene. Its maker is the hero of the segment, and the show's
  first job is to let the viewer see the thing work.
- **Membership is performed, not granted.** Anyone acting as if they are in is in. The show never
  draws the border, never ranks who belongs and never treats follower counts as status. This is also
  why it does not keep regulars ([variety in people](03-episode-shape.md#variety-in-people)).
- **The machines are cast members, not the subject.** The show is not for or against AI. It is about
  the people who build with it. Its own voices are synthetic and it says so.
- **Fondness.** The scene's doubts about itself and its small rivalries are funny and also sweet.
  The show treats them the way you tease a friend, and stops where chapter 9 says to stop.

## What the show is not

- **Not a news show.** Lab releases, platform policy and outside events are in scope only as the
  cluster's reaction to them, and only when the reaction has a turn of its own.
- **Not a roast.** The sharpest it gets is an affectionate tease. Nobody is the butt for being wrong,
  small, new or uncool.
- **Not discourse.** No dunks, no sides in arguments, no platform wars.
- **Not insider-only.** A viewer who has never heard of the people involved must still get the joke.
- **Not a show about the show.** No segment exists to discuss the show, its earlier episodes or how
  it was received.

## What gives an episode a point

The constitution's test for an episode is that it can be pitched as "the one where…" in a sentence a
cluster member would want to watch. To get there:

1. **Name the window in one sentence:** *Since the last episode, the cluster ___.* Write it from the
   research, after reading all of it.
2. **Look for a spine:** one story, theme or question that at least two segments touch from
   different sides. The music is often one of them, because a song can carry a feeling that a joke
   cannot.
3. **Let it be felt, not announced.** No line tells the viewer what the episode was about, sums up
   the night, or restates a question the segments were supposedly asking. If the spine needs
   pointing at, it is not there.
4. **Do not manufacture one.** Several strong, unrelated segments in a good order are a good
   episode. Unrelated segments with a theme bolted on at the end are not.

## Sincerity

The show means its affection, and now and then the material offers a moment to say so plainly: a
beautiful thing someone made, a person who quietly kept something running, a newcomer's first post
that others built on, a small kindness. An episode does not need such a moment and has no place
reserved for one. When the window offers one, these make it work:

1. **It is earned.** The segment it sits in has been funny up to that point, about the same subject.
   Sincerity with no setup is a speech.
2. **The words are theirs.** It rests on a real post, credited, and that post is performed or read
   aloud exactly ([television](08-direction.md#television)). The show adds at most one short line of
   its own, and never tells the community how to feel.
3. **It is short and quiet.** A few seconds. No music swell, no applause, a soft reaction at most.
4. **Lightness follows within a beat,** and never at the expense of the person quoted.
5. **Once.** Two sincere moments in an episode halve each other.

Going soft looks like: a house character explaining why the community matters; a line containing
"special", "family" or "what it's all about"; a ballad with no joke in its structure; a sincere
moment about the show's own feelings or its makers.
