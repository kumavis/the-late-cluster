# 6. Joke craft, for this show

## The house shape: fact, turn, punch, picture

Almost every joke in the pilot has four parts:

1. **Fact.** A true thing from the week, said quickly and plainly.
2. **Turn.** A second fact or a reframing that points somewhere unexpected.
3. **Punch.** Short, slower, last word strongest.
4. **Picture.** Something lands on screen on the punch word, usually carrying a second joke in text
   that nobody says.

> "The simcluster is named after a Twitter algorithm." (fact) / "Bluesky doesn't even use it."
> (turn; a NOT INSTALLED stamp lands) / "So we're a tribute band." (punch; cut to the band nodding
> along, chyron *Hy Oid & The Formants · A tribute band*)

> "The book club said no to a Discord." (fact; a Discord icon, stamped NO) / "Membership is supposed
> to be fuzzy." (turn; MEMBERSHIP: FUZZY ✓ / DISCORD: NOT FUZZY ✗) / "So now there are four
> Discords." (punch; three more pop on the word "four") ... then a real reply card: *I'm in like
> three simcluster discords… But I'd be down to join a fourth.* (the picture is the topper)

When you write a joke, write all four. If you can't say what the picture is, the joke isn't
finished.

## Why the screen carries the density

The synthesized voice is slow, a little smeared, and loses one word in five (see
[voices](07-voices.md)). Text on screen is crisp. So the show splits the work:

- **The voice says the setup and the punch,** in short common words.
- **The screen carries the tags**: the second and third jokes, the numbers, the provenance.

Pilot examples of jokes that exist only on screen: the museum placard *EXHIBIT 31 · TALK SHOW
HOST, ASKING A QUESTION · c. 1962 · MIXED MEDIA (TUXEDO) · PLEASE DO NOT TAP THE GLASS*; the
weather footnote *OUR MODEL: ONE LARYNX*; the site that shipped mid-sentence, *STATUS: IT IS MORNING
SOMEWHERE*; the Hamilton card *THE MUSICAL · NOW IN ITS FIRST MINUTE*; the sports ticker (*Chicken
declines to comment*); the credits (*Podcast name: [ field left blank ]*). Rule: **every graphic
carries at least one line of text that is a joke the voice does not say.**

## Specificity

Real beats invented. Small beats big. Named beats generic.

| generic (don't) | specific (the pilot) |
|---|---|
| "Everybody's building a weather app now." | "The simcluster has a weather channel now. / Naturally, it only forecasts itself." with the real 177 and 29 posts per hour |
| "The bots are winning." | "A bot is up nineteen thousand dollars. / Humans are down four dollars." with +$19,865.83 on a split-flap |
| "There's a podcast." | "Twelve episodes so far." / "What is it called?" / "It doesn't have a name." |
| "Abel replies to norvid a lot." | a pie chart growing to 39%, *389 OF 995 POSTS*, an alligator under the slice |

Get the specifics from the digest. Never invent a number about a real person or project; if the
digest doesn't have it, use a joke that doesn't need it.

## Escalation

Establish a pattern, then break it, then break the break. Built or Bit? is the model:

1. Real → BUILT. Real → BUILT. (The audience learns the rule.)
2. BIT → built after the ruling: "That took four seconds!"
3. BIT → built during the ruling: "Bit," / "Built it." (overlapping) / "She didn't even finish!"
4. BIT → built as a physical object: the E key drops onto the desk with a thud.
5. BIT → built, and it's the room: "Wait. It's this show."

Each step changes one variable (timing, then size, then scope). A five-step escalation is the
most a segment can hold; three is usual for a weekly.

## The runner: Built it.

"Built it." is the show's signature and its biggest risk. Fifteen identical punchlines are
not a runner, they are a tic. The pilot never does the same one twice. Use this table to pick a
shape, and don't repeat a shape within an episode.

| shape | how | pilot |
|---|---|---|
| plain | a wish, a beat, "Built it." | cold open; monologue ("This monologue was a joke an hour ago.") |
| pre-empted | someone else has already built it | Hy: "Already built it, Larry." |
| mid-sentence | it ships while the line is still going | "It shipped during that sentence.." / "Built it." |
| interrupting | overlaps the ruling | "Bit," / "Built it." / "She didn't even finish!" |
| protested | a character objects | "Hey! That was hypothetical!" |
| physical | the built thing arrives in the studio | the E key; the crow out of the machine |
| on the host | the build happens to Larry | Larry in a museum case |
| doubled | one wish, two builds | "Built it." / "Both of them?" |
| the reply | the bot answers a question with a fact | "When?" / "You are in it." |
| contradicting | the tally is wrong the moment it's read | "fourteen things built" → the gn checker → 15 |
| useless | it builds exactly what was asked, and it doesn't help | the gn checker, which says NO |

New shapes are welcome: built too early (before the wish), built wrong, built by the audience,
the bot asked to build something and refusing on ethics (callback to the vote-rigging refusal).

## Characters make jokes, not just lines

Give a punch to whoever the fact bothers or delights most:

- Eppy protests the bot because hypotheticals are his whole sport.
- Larry gets "That was my four dollars." because he is always the loser.
- Sibyl gets "You're already enrolled." because she knows the rules better than you.
- The bot gets every wish because building is all it does.

A real person's line becomes a punch only when the show's setup recreates the occasion they said
it on (R4); otherwise the house characters take the punches around it.

## Demonstrate, don't describe

"Everyone's favorite bot has exactly one setting." would be a description. The pilot then cuts to
Sibyl doing it ("Yeah absolutely."), Larry says "Now everybody talks like that.", and the band
answers in unison ("Agreed yes yeah totally!"). Showing the behaviour twice is the joke. The same
goes for the engine: "nobody says gn" becomes Sibyl actually trying to say it.

## Buttons

Each segment ends on a short line, usually Larry's, six words or fewer, deadpan, tagging the
segment's main idea or calling back a number:

- "Thank you, Samantha. Bring a book, everybody."
- "Thanks, Eppy. / That was my four dollars."
- "Do not say spin-off."
- "So. gn, simcluster."

## The pilot's best jokes, and why

1. **"She founded the chicken. She cannot be the chicken."** A real rule that disqualifies its own
   founder. Parallel construction, one word changed. Staged: the founder walks up and bonks the
   limbo bar. Specific, affectionate, absurd.
2. **"So now there are four Discords."** The rule of the setup defeats itself, and a real post
   confirms it with a topper.
3. **"Both weeks were on Monday."** A real habit (declaring themed weeks) with a factual twist,
   tagged on screen with a stamp: TOTAL DURATION: 1 DAY.
4. **Exhibit 31.** Larry in a glass case with a placard (*TALK SHOW HOST, ASKING A QUESTION · c. 1962
   · MIXED MEDIA (TUXEDO) · PLEASE DO NOT TAP THE GLASS*): the joke is all in the staging.
5. **The song's stops.** "Both of them?" / "When?" / "You are in it." The form of the song is the
   form of the joke, and the last answer is the show's thesis.
6. **"Two to three inches of assigned reading."** Genre vocabulary plus community content, with a
   stack of books growing on the map. (In the pilot Samantha's puppet said it; now a house presenter
   would.)
7. **"Hey! That was hypothetical!"** The only character who objects to the runner, which makes the
   runner funnier.

One of those big laughs holds under today's rules and one doesn't. *buddy that's my freaking
simcluster* as norvid's reply to "My beautiful city" passes: he said it as that kind of reply to that
kind of remark, so the staged exchange recreates the occasion (R4). "claim." as his reply to "Thank you
for coming" fails: he doesn't use it to answer greetings. The lesson: the setup is the show's, and it
must be the kind of prompt the real line was answering.

## The pilot's weaker jokes, and what to do instead

- **"I scored a sixty one. / My mom says I'm a hundred."** A generic template that would work for
  any quiz; the groan sound admits it. The setup had something better on screen: the site's real
  footer says *Your score is a function of who started the crawl, not who you are*. A specific
  button would use that ("I started the crawl. I still got a sixty one.").
- **"Vibes remain immeasurable."** Connective tissue, not a joke. Fine once; don't build on it.
- **"Even the crow came down for this."** Filler while the crow lands. The landing was the joke; the
  line adds nothing. Let the picture play with a chuckle, or give the crow a caw as the punch.
- **"And we didn't even play upping my p(doom)."** Depends on a reference the episode never sets up.
  The safety board flipping from 3 to 0 rescues it visually. Set up the reference earlier or cut it.
- **"abelian simply lives under his posts (like an alligator) (respectfully)".** The parentheses are
  the joke in text and vanish in speech (the `as` strips them). When a post's humour is
  typographic, show it on a card and have the guest say something else.
- **"Simcluster Hamilton!" / "That took four seconds!"** Restates the runner. Acceptable as the first
  break in an escalation; it would be weak anywhere else.
- **"A hot dog toppings tier list!"** Unclear whether this came from the week's posts. If a card in
  Built or Bit? isn't from the cluster, it is just a random idea, and the format's point is lost.

## Anti-patterns

**Reference without a joke.** Naming a thing and waiting for recognition.
> Don't: "And of course, catoid week!" (applause)
> Do: "Big week, folks. Catoid week rolled right into chimera vocal synth week. / Both weeks were on
> Monday."

**Explaining the meme.** If you need three lines to explain it, it is not a setup, it is a lecture.
Pick a different story, or let a graphic do the explaining while the voice does the joke.

**Restating a post.** Larry reads a funny post aloud and reacts. The post was already the joke; the
show added nothing. Instead keep the post as it was and put the joke around it: a house character's
setup or topper (the Discords reply after "So now there are four Discords."), a genre frame the
house presents (Samantha's gn post beside the forecast's gn chances), or a build the post wished for.
Or give it a setup that recreates the occasion it was said on (R4). Never a context that changes
what it meant.

**The audience as the punchline.** "Am I right, simcluster?" Jokes about the viewers' habits in
general ("you all post too much") are lazy. Use one specific instance from the week.

**Dunking.** On a person, a rival platform, a lab, a bad take. Off-tone, and it ages badly.

**Generic AI jokes.** "The robots are taking over." The cluster has heard them all. The show's AI
jokes are about specific bots doing specific things (one setting, opt-in only, refusing a vote).

**Meta too often.** The show is about itself in the cold open, in "Wait. It's this show." and in
"You are in it." That was the pilot introducing itself. In a weekly, one meta joke an episode at
most, and never the same reveal twice.

**Engine-dependent punchlines.** A punch on a word the engine can't say ("chicken", "claim",
"joke", "feed", "live" at the end of a phrase). See [voices](07-voices.md). The caption saves it
only partly.

**Long setups.** More than two lines before the turn. Cut the first line; it is usually context the
card can carry.

## A checklist for each joke

- [ ] Is the fact true and in the digest (or the show's lore)?
- [ ] Is there a turn, and can I name it in five words?
- [ ] Is the punch the last word or two, and is it short?
- [ ] What lands on screen, and on which word?
- [ ] Does the graphic carry a tag the voice doesn't say?
- [ ] Would a viewer who missed the week get it from the line and the card?
- [ ] Would the person in it repost it?
- [ ] Can the engine say the punch word?
