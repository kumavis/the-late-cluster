# 003 · The Walk Home (a holiday special)

**Drafted, not aired.** Made on 27 November 2026, on the branch `episode/003-holiday-2026`, as a
holiday special the owner outlined. It has no window: it uses no posts. 4:11.1 (251.1 s), then
plain credits of about 19 s. 403 spoken words (369 of them Larry's) and 11 sung syllables. One
segment, `walk`. The owner has watched the video, called it good, and had four things changed;
this record is of the special as it stands after them. The audience has not seen any of it.

Source: `episodes/003-holiday-2026/` (`segments/walk/script.js` for the words; `index.js` and one
file per place for the staging; `production/` for the working papers, with the owner's outline,
notes and rulings in `production/owner-notes.md`). The preview build is
`preview/003-holiday-2026/`. Machine-readable summary: [003-holiday-2026.json](003-holiday-2026.json).

For reviewers and the project manager only. Writers, researchers and builders do not read this file.

The special in one sentence: there is no show tonight, so Larry, off the air and in an overcoat,
walks the viewer home through a cold city, tells what an old cook, an old rabbi and an old
couplet knew, and cannot leave the viewer on the pavement outside his own dinner.

- **Nobody real appears.** No guest, no post, no account on screen, nothing from a window. The
  tracker does not change.
- **No song.** A busker sings a wordless tune that stops one note short.
- **No audience.** No laugh, applause or sting anywhere. The sounds are footsteps, a room heard
  through glass, clicks, a room of friends.
- **No standing segment, no title card, no announcer.**
- **Sincerity is the piece.** Four minutes in Larry's own words. That is the experiment.

## How it came about

**The owner's outline** (27 November 2026, in `production/owner-notes.md`): the studio switched
off; Larry off the air, more honest and personal, slower to joke, dressed for the cold, picking
up his paycheck; "would you walk with me a bit?"; a scarf; cold dark streets; he talks as in
conversation, the audience never speaks, and he repeats the question to give it a voice; he
explains what thinkers from different cultures said, to comfort and not to show off; restaurants
and buskers; his apartment, a big dinner with the regular crew, the holiday never named; "its a
crazy mixed up world out there, keep the ones that make you feel sane close"; "stay warm folks,
goodnight". About four minutes. First "this is not episode 3", then "we can call this episode 3
for simplicity".

**The owner's five lines,** kept with only punctuation tidied: "Oh, no show tonight, folks.
Holidays and all." / "Just here picking up my paycheck." / "Would you walk with me a bit?" /
"It's a crazy mixed up world out there." with "Keep the ones that make you feel sane close."
(one sentence taken as two beats) / "Stay warm, folks. Goodnight."

**How it was made.** By hand through the workflow's roles, because the pipeline runs whole weeks
from a scrape. One researcher wrote eighteen cards on old texts, each checked against a published
translation. Three writers drafted alone. Two reviewers scored the drafts and ran the repetition
gate; one auditor checked every attribution. One writer assembled the final script from the
second draft and parts of the other two. Two reviewers and the auditor passed it. The owner ruled
on the rabbi and said to go on to the video. A sound editor paced and fitted it; six builders
staged it (a lead, four scene builders and one for the clothes); an auditor passed the frames;
six cold viewers were shown stills, and three more after the staging changed. The final review
approved it for the owner's watch.

**After the owner's watch** ("good. couple weak visual details"), four things changed, all at
the owner's word:

1. *The side street at the crossing.* The far skyline showed through it. It now ends in one
   plain building.
2. *The bowls.* Small bowls slid along the table by themselves. Now nothing slides along it:
   the bowl that is served to us stands at Larry's left in one shot and is at our plate after a
   cut, and the bowls of the meal are gone. Asked about the second chair and the place setting,
   the owner said "Leave it sliding".
3. *Sibyl.* She sat small under a tall chair back with her head down through her line. She now
   sits at the head of the table in an open chair, and lifts her head to look at Larry when she
   says "It's still the rule."
4. *The start of the walk.* "a little too much negativity, or focus on it. things are changing
   fast, horrors are witnessed, incredible joys are experienced. a lot is happening." A writer
   drafted three versions and the owner chose the third: the line "Things move fast lately. And
   fair is hard to find." is gone, and Larry tells it on himself instead.

Two sound fixes were made between the review and the watch: "joke" is heard as itself, and no
layer of the room starts during the owner's last lines.

**The feeling of the close.** Both final reviewers put this to the owner and it is still open:
both aired episodes ended with the house taking Larry in after he had put himself outside it,
and then turning to the viewer. This one takes the viewer in, and with
`started-without-me` ("They'll have started without me.", answered by "Nobody has started.") it
takes Larry in as well. The mechanism is new in every part (no song, no stop, no lights, no
note, a rule of hospitality and a chair). It would still be the third close of three with that
feeling.

**What the rewrite thinned,** from the second-round reviewers, with which the final review
agrees. Nobody now says that things are fast, so the cook who slows down answers only "Same
hour." and the hurry said and shown at the window. And "What if none of it ever gets put right?"
has only "the bad way" to point back to, so the owner's "justice seems rare" is carried by that
one question, which comes from us. One gain: good and bad in the same hour sets up the owner's
"crazy mixed up world" in the first minute.

## Place by place

One segment and one unbroken walk. Times are from the top, from the cut as it stands.

### 1. The dark studio (0:00, 22.4 s)

**Shape.** The set switched off, held in silence; he notices us; two of the owner's lines; a
confession; the ask; our yes taken; the scarf; the door; the lamp off; black.

**Spoke:** Larry.

**Lines that work.**
1. *No show.* "Oh, no show tonight, folks. Holidays and all." His head comes up from the desk
   to us in the doorway. The first words of the special, with no title before them.
2. *The pretext.* "Just here picking up my paycheck." (a blank envelope leaning on his mug) /
   "They'd mail it. I like the walk home." Premise: he came in for an envelope. Turn: they
   would have posted it. Picture: the envelope is now at his coat pocket. The line gives the
   special its title and makes the owner's line before it a pretext.
3. *The ask.* "Would you walk with me a bit?" / 1.4 s / "Good. Let me get my scarf." The
   pause is our yes.

**Staging.** A wide of the studio barely there: truss, curtain, couch, bandstand, desk, the
sign and ON AIR dark and blank, a crescent moon and the city in the window. One work lamp, a
reflector on a stand above his head with a cone of light to a pool by the desk. Larry side-on
in a long rumpled taupe overcoat with the collar up, no tuxedo, no bow tie. A close on the desk;
a medium on him. He looks down; a cut to the collar with the gold scarf nearly on, and it
settles. The exit door swings open on the dark. The lamp goes out. Black for most of a second.

**Sound.** An empty room. One car passing outside (a soft whoosh, 1.5). The lamp: a voiced
tongue click (21.6).

### 2. The street (0:22, 33.9 s)

**Shape.** Walking with nobody talking; he asks us something and agrees with an answer nobody
hears; what "me too" means for him, told on himself; no joke for it; the old books; the first
say-back and an honest small answer.

**Spoke:** Larry.

**Lines that work.**
1. *The unheard answer.* "So. How have you been?" / 2.4 s of footsteps, his head still turned
   to us / a small late nod / "Yeah. Me too." The shared unease is set down with neither of
   us naming it.
2. *One sentence for both.* I keep saying, "I can't believe it." / "The good way and the bad
   way. Same hour." Premise: a man short of words. Turn: the one sentence he has is meant two
   opposite ways. He describes himself and never the world. The owner's note asked for the
   horrors and the joys together; this is all of it, and nothing is named or shown.
3. *No joke.* "I don't have a joke for that." / "I looked." "That" is now the good and the
   bad arriving together. Punch: two words. Both select reviewers wanted these kept.
4. "So I've been reading old books. Very old." / "They couldn't believe it, either. Some wrote
   down what helped." His own phrase handed to the people in the books, which is his reason
   for reading them.
5. *The first say-back.* He turns and holds; "Does it help?" / he looks back at the road /
   "Some nights."

**Staging.** A tracking shot, chest up: Larry side-on at the left third, walking screen-right
with a small bob a step and a puff of breath every few steps. The street slides left in layers
at different speeds: a far skyline, dark fronts with a few lit windows, lamp posts each drawn
again upside down and dimmer in the wet road, the kerb. The camera is us at his shoulder. One
framing for the whole stretch; from 32.9 to 52.2 he is in profile, under six lines.

**Sound.** Two sets of footsteps: lip pops from the band's percussion voices, his on every
beat at 102 a minute and a little right, ours softer, uneven and central, so they drift in
and out of step. A car goes by (24.6).

### 3. The restaurant, and the cook (0:56, 34.9 s). The first idea

**Shape.** A warm window slides in and he stops; what he stops for; he tells an old story
about a cook; the cook in the hatch does it on the word; nobody says what it means.

**Spoke:** Larry. **Told from:** the Zhuangzi, chapter 3, the cook who carved the ox. Told,
not quoted; the ox is left out.

**Lines that work.**
1. "I always stop here." / "One cook in back, feeding the whole room." Since the rewrite of
   the street this is the only spoken hurry the cook's story answers.
2. "There's a story about a cook in an old Chinese book." / "The Zhuangzi. I'm probably saying
   that wrong." The engine does say it wrong, so the hedge is true.
3. "The way I remember it, a prince admires his cook's knife work." / "The cook says he's had
   one blade nineteen years. Still sharp."
4. *The cook slows.* "When he comes to a hard part, he slows down." On "slows down" the cook
   in the hatch stops, leans in over the board, and goes on slowly, while the dining room
   bobs at full speed in front of him. "Looks right at it. Goes gently." Then 3.4 s with
   nothing said: Larry at the glass at the left edge, watching, and the cook going slowly.
   A draft's line that said what it meant ("I keep that one.") was cut.

**Staging.** Amber light. A big front window on our side of the street: every table taken,
head-and-shoulder silhouettes bobbing, two hanging lamps, a small pink neon OPEN, and at the
back a lit kitchen hatch with one cook's silhouette in a tall hat, a plate on the pass and
steam rising past the hatch's frame. A push in to the hatch. A close on Larry at the glass,
turned to us, the room beside him. The hatch closer. A wide for the hold. The neon is drawn
only when all of it is in frame.

**Sound.** A full room through glass: one murmur bed from 56.4 to 91.2, rising as we come
level, under the speech, and sliding off left with the window. No footsteps while we stand.

### 4. The busker (1:31, 24.8 s)

**Shape.** Heard before he is seen; we stop; the tune stops one note short; a coin; a
thank-you said to him and not to us; walking on, one line about him; he starts again behind
us; one more line.

**Spoke:** Larry. **Sang:** the busker, twice, with no words and no caption.

**Lines that work.**
1. *The stop.* Seven notes that climb and stop on a note that is not home. His head tilts, as
   if looking for the one after it. 1.4 s of true silence. A coin lands in the case.
   "That's a good one. Thank you."
2. *Started anyway.* "Nobody there till we came by. He'd started anyway." The outline asks
   Larry to say that we keep making things. He never says it; this is where it is shown.
3. *Never ends.* Behind us he starts the tune over, small and far left. Under its last note:
   "He never does get to the end of it." Said fondly, to the road ahead.

**Staging.** A cut that jumps the walk on, so the restaurant is a sliver at the left edge and
the street is dark when the tune is heard. A man under the only lamp in his stretch: a short
violet coat, collar up, a brimmed hat, an open case at his feet with nothing in it, a small
note glyph by his head while he sings, an empty pavement both ways. A two-shot; a close on him
as the tune stops. The coin rises from just below the frame in front of Larry, arcs and lands;
no hand. He slides away left, getting smaller.

**Sound.** The tune: seven notes in G that end on A, at 72 a minute, in an invented voice of
its own (about 145 Hz, panned right), at full speech level. The coin: a bright unpitched
click and its bounce. The second phrase: four notes, quiet, far left.

### 5. The crossing, and the rabbi (1:56, 50.6 s). The second idea

**Shape.** A red sign on an empty road; he answers a look; our hardest question, held and
said back; he does not know; a rabbi's sentence, exact; silence; where he found it; whose it
is; how he borrows it; the street does not answer; the sign changes in its own time.

**Spoke:** Larry. **Quoted:** Rabbi Tarfon, in Pirkei Avot, chapter 2, in Joseph I.
Gorfinkle's translation (*The Sayings of the Jewish Fathers*, first published 1913), exactly.

**Lines that work.**
1. *The look.* "No cars, I know. I wait anyway." We have looked up the empty road and back
   at him. Nothing is said back.
2. *The question.* A close, the red of the sign on him. He turns to us slowly and stays, for
   2.2 s. "What if none of it ever gets put right?" / he looks across at the red /
   "I don't know. I really don't." Since the rewrite this is the first and only place the
   special says anything is wrong enough to need putting right, and it is we who say it.
3. *The sentence.* "Nearly two thousand years ago there was a rabbi named Tarfon." / "He is
   recorded as saying:" / "It is not thy duty to complete the work, but neither art thou free
   to desist from it." Said plainly, in two breaths at its comma. 2.6 s of silence.
4. *His own.* "I found it in a used book. Somebody had already underlined it." / "He meant
   the study of Torah. I'm a guest in that sentence."
5. *The borrowing.* "Here's how I borrow it." / "We don't have to finish it." / "We just
   don't get to quit." The plain form of the idea, and the two lines a viewer would repeat.
6. *The street does not answer.* The sign stays red for 3.4 s after his last word. He looks
   at it, then at us, with a small tilt of the head. Then WALK, in green, on one plain tick,
   on no word. Nobody looks up at it. A draft had it change on his last word and gave him
   "I didn't arrange that."; both select reviewers said the street was applauding him.

**Staging.** Six framings at one kerb: a wide as they arrive; a medium for the look, DON'T
WALK large on its post; a close tinted red with the sign out of frame; closer still for the
quotation, the sign a red blur; a looser medium; the wide again. A side street whose lamps
recede to one plain building that closes it, a door and two windows, one lit; nothing far
shows through it. The road is empty in every frame. The only words on screen are the sign's.

**Sound.** Nothing. From 116.1 to 166.7 there is true silence between the lines, and one
tick when the sign changes.

### 6. The snow and the last block (2:47, 11.6 s)

**Shape.** They cross; it starts to snow; five seconds with no word; one line said straight
ahead; a glance.

**Lines that work.** "Nearly there. I usually walk this block on my own." He says it looking
ahead. Then his head turns to us and away again at once. A draft followed it with a line from
an old Norse poem about finding a comrade; that was cut, and the glance carries it.

**Staging.** Tracking again, the green sign sliding behind him. A few flakes from the middle
of the road, then more; it never stops after this. A closer framing on the last block, rows
of lit flat windows above him, nobody else on the pavement.

**Sound.** Four steps off the kerb, then both sets softer in the snow.

### 7. His door, and the couplet (2:58, 25.3 s). The third idea

**Shape.** His building, and a warm crowded window at the top; he expects they have started;
he turns to go in and we have not moved; the third say-back; he answers it with a book,
looking up at the window; he is caught wanting us to come; the door opens from upstairs.

**Spoke:** Larry. **Told from:** the Tirukkural, chapter 9, couplet 82. Told, not quoted.

**Lines that work.**
1. "This is me." / "They'll have started without me." He is wrong.
2. *The say-back.* He turns to the door, stops, turns back; 2 s; "Should you come up?"
3. *The excuse.* "There's an old Tamil book of couplets called the Tirukkural." / "As I
   remember it: even with the food of immortality on your table," / "you don't eat while a
   guest stands outside." (the picture is the window, close: one large figure and four small
   heads at a table under a lamp) / he looks at us / "So you see my position." Premise: an
   old text offered as wisdom. Turn: it is his reason for not leaving us outside. The one
   idea he does and does not tell.
4. *Let in.* Before he can turn, the latch goes and the door swings in. Warm light across the
   snow and across him. The figure that stood at the top window has gone from it. "Come on up."

**Staging.** A cut that jumps the walk on, so his building is in place in the wide. A brick
front in the snow, a street door with a lit pane over it, three floors of dark windows, and
one top-floor window warm with silhouettes: the restaurant picture again, smaller. Larry
small at the door, head tipped up. A closer shot at the door for the question. A close on the
window for the couplet. The brick warms when the door opens.

### 8. The table (3:24, 28.2 s to his turn)

**Shape.** A burst of talk cut off; six heads turn to the door; three lines that say he is
late and that they waited; he says he had company; all six look at us; one word; a chair; we
sit; a rule; we are served first; he objects and loses; one friend's laugh; the meal goes on
and nobody looks at us.

**Spoke:** Sibyl (three lines), Pete, Hy, Larry, Al, Eppy, Dee (a laugh).

**Lines that work.**
1. *They waited.* Sibyl, not raising her voice: "You're late. Nobody has started." Every
   plate is clean and the dish is covered. Four words tell us they waited, and that he was
   wrong at the door.
2. Pete: "I counted you in twice." What a drummer says to someone who missed his entrance.
   The first line he has spoken alone. Hy: "You took the long way." He knows where Larry
   has been.
3. "I had company." Said to us, beside him in the doorway, snow on his shoulders. It answers
   the last block.
4. *Chair.* All six heads turn from Larry to the camera and hold for two seconds, in a room
   whose light does not change. Al: "Chair." A second chair slides in beside the gold one.
   The camera goes to it and settles at table height. A plate, a glass and a fork slide in
   along the bottom of the frame. Premise: a stranger at the door of a table of friends.
   Turn: one word and furniture.
5. *The rule.* Sibyl: "The guest is served first. That is the rule." The dish has lost its
   lid at the cut. A bowl stands at Larry's left, just handed along, and his head is turned
   to it; after a cut it is at our plate and he is looking down at it there. It has gone past
   him. Larry: "It's my house." Sibyl is at her plate while he says it. Then she lifts her
   head, turns to him, unhurried and level, and says: "It's still the rule." His own couplet
   done back to him 24 seconds after he told it. The best laugh in the special, and in its
   back half.
6. Eppy, very straight, to the table: "And the ruling stands!" Dee: "Ha!", one voice, no
   caption. The cut test finds this line; it is the owner's either-or.
7. *The meal.* From our chair. Everyone talks at once, there is food on every plate and on
   ours, nothing moves along the table, the life is in the heads, and nobody is looking at
   us any more, which is the welcome.

**Staging.** A warm brown room: a lamp, a window with snow going past it, one long table
drawn in front of all six so that it hides them below the chest, candles, glasses that nobody
lifts, one big covered dish. From the door: Pete, Hy, Dee, Al and Eppy along the far side and
Sibyl at its right end, the head, in an open wooden chair; Larry at the frame's edge. On the
near side one empty chair with its back to us, in gold. Seven heads share the frame only in
the two wides from the door. From the chair on, every shot is from our seat and holds four
heads or fewer; Larry is beside us in the gold chair, coat gone, an oatmeal cardigan, the
scarf still on. Uncovered, the dish is a brown loaf with steam; nobody names it. The serving
is two shots with a cut between them (221.2), and the bowl is still in both.

**Sound.** Six people talking over each other (one loud murmur) and a gasp for the breath
they stop on; near silence for the look; the room starts up again, warmer, when the chair
comes in, and runs as a bed to the end. No sound of the chair: the sound editor could not
make one that read as a chair.

## The experiment

**What.** Four earnest minutes in Larry's own words, outside the studio, with no posts, no
guests and no audience. It tests the premise chapter's rules for sincerity (once, short,
earned by comedy, in the community's own words) and asks whether the show can hold a sincere
piece of its own without it becoming a speech. The owner set the form. It changes several
things at once, so a result will say whether the whole held and not which part did it.

**The measures** (two reviewers', unchanged): shown once with sound to three people who have
not read it, at least two can say back one thing Larry offered that a person could do tonight
and what happened when he got home; at least two give the don't-have-to-finish, don't-get-to-
quit idea in their own words; at least two name something that happens and not something they
were told to feel; none can point at a line of Larry's own that told them how to feel; none
calls it a speech, a lesson or a list; and its cues hold no laugh, applause or sting.

**Before air.** The last clause holds: the cues are 2 whoosh, 6 tick, 42 murmur and 1 gasp.
The measures themselves have not been run. Two things stand in for them so far.

- *The owner's watch.* The owner watched the video and called it good, and then asked for the
  start of the walk to dwell less on the negative. That is one person, who had read the
  script; it is the first watch by anyone.
- *Cold viewers on stills.* Six, each shown one stretch that carries meaning without a word.
  They named the place and the man every time, the sign changing, the door opening and a meal
  being served. They did not see the snow, took the work lamp for a microphone and the kitchen
  hatch for a screen; all three were changed, and three new viewers then read the lamp as a
  lamp, saw the snow, and named a kitchen hatch. They did not see the figure at the top window
  or that six people were looking at us; neither was changed or shown again. Stills cannot
  show the cook's change of pace at all.

Scored 1 of 2 at the final review, and again in its addendum: built in whole, measure not yet
run.

**After air.** Nothing yet.

**What changed in the bible.** Nothing. See `production/dna-amendments.md`.

## Anything that broke or bent a rule, found at review

Nothing was broken without the owner's hand on it. These are the rules the special sets aside
or leans on, so that a later reviewer does not take them for precedent.

- **Sincerity for four minutes in the show's own words.** The owner's outline. The premise
  chapter's rules stand for a weekly episode.
- **No posts, no people, no standing segments.** The owner's outline, for this special only.
- **Religion named and quoted.** Chapter 9's R10 keeps religion out even as a reference. The
  owner ruled "Keep the rabbi" for this special. The project manager takes that to cover the
  Zhuangzi and the Tirukkural told as old books. It changes nothing about R10 for the people
  the show covers.
- **A house character explaining what a real person thought.** Article 3 forbids it of the
  people the show covers. The outline asks for it of thinkers long dead. The project manager
  wrote the reading down for the owner to confirm; the owner said to go ahead and has not
  answered it in so many words.
- **The short list.** The special's first briefs listed the out-of-bounds subjects from the
  bible's one-page summary, which does not name religion. Both final reviewers caught it; the
  select reviewers, the auditor and the project manager had not.
- **A real saying heard as noise.** The quotation's first half is transcribed as "It is not
  lied to it to compete to the work". Accepted under the owner's ruling that best effort is
  sufficient because the subtitles carry the line; it is exact in the caption as it is said.
- **Heard text.** 37 of 60 spoken lines are listed as still heard differently in at least one
  take. The three names are not heard as themselves. "eat" and "had" are heard as "hit";
  "walk" cannot be said and is in three lines, one of them the owner's; "bad" in the new
  `both-ways` is heard as "best" in two takes of four, which turns the line round. Two things
  were heard that are never accepted, and both were changed: an early spelling of the cook
  line (crude), and "joke" as "drug", which the final review listed and the sound editor then
  cured with a twelfth spelling. Nothing kept is crude, alarming or out of bounds.
- **No title card and plain credits.** The project manager's reading of "go ahead". The
  constitution's credits are all there.
- **Process.** The repetition record for the script was written from the two final reviews
  (each judged every line and gave the whole script the verdict fresh); the three lines of the
  rewrite went through a second round of the gate proper. The final audit has two rounds, the
  second on those three lines. The stage auditor's frames are of the cut before the rewrite;
  only the final reviewer has looked at frames of the new lines.

## Notes for the next gate

For a reviewer of the next episode. None of this goes to a writer except as an `avoid` line.
While this is unaired, read these as warnings and not as blocks.

- **The close.** Two aired episodes ended with the house taking the host in. This would be a
  third ending with that feeling, by a new mechanism, and it takes the viewer in too. Whatever
  the owner decides about `started-without-me`, a fourth such ending is a signature however
  it is built.
- **The house as friends.** A table of the house cast as close friends, off duty and in their
  own clothes, with the viewer among them, is a picture the show can only make once. A later
  sincere beat about the house's own fondness for itself would repeat it.
- **The viewer as a companion.** 002's making-of had the host keep his manners toward his own
  camera as it went in. Here the camera is a person for four minutes: walked with, asked,
  answered, seated and served. A third piece built on the camera being someone is a habit.
- **Voicing an absent person's question.** 002 had "A member asked". This has three questions
  said back. Different jobs; the same act.
- **The host quoting what he says.** 002: "Me, I just say, good question." Here: I keep
  saying, "I can't believe it." A third is a habit.
- **A contrast, then "Same" and a noun.** 002's sports desk ("Same title."); here "Same
  hour." Both gate reviewers passed it as ordinary clipped speech and one said plainly that a
  third line ending on it would make it a form.
- **A phrase brought back with its person changed.** Spent as a guest's phrase returned as a

- **A tune or chord that stops wanting one more note.** 002's vamp; this busker.
