The special in one sentence: the show's logo flickers and goes out, there is no show tonight, so
Larry, off the air and in an overcoat, walks the viewer home through a cold city, tells what an
old cook and an old rabbi knew, lets slip one line of an old poem about being glad of company,
and cannot leave the viewer on the pavement outside his own dinner.

- **Nobody real appears.** No guest, no post, no account on screen, nothing from a window. The
  tracker does not change.
- **No song.** A busker sings a wordless tune that stops one note short.
- **No audience.** No laugh, applause or sting anywhere. The sounds are footsteps, a room heard
  through glass, clicks, a room of friends.
- **No standing segment, no announcer, no stock title card.** It opens on the show's logo
  going out.
- **Sincerity is the piece.** Four minutes and a bit in Larry's own words. That is the experiment.
- **Four old texts:** two told in his words (a Chinese story, a Tamil couplet) and two quoted
  exactly from named translations (a rabbi's sentence, one line of a Norse poem).
- **One turned item:** the opening. Everything else is fresh.

## How it came about

**The owner's outline** (27 November 2026, in `production/owner-notes.md`): the studio switched
off; Larry off the air, more honest and personal, slower to joke, dressed for the cold, picking
up his paycheck; "would you walk with me a bit?"; a scarf; cold dark streets; he talks as in
conversation, the audience never speaks, and he repeats the question to give it a voice; he
explains what thinkers from different cultures said, to comfort and not to show off; restaurants
and buskers; his apartment, a big dinner with the regular crew, the holiday never named; "its a
crazy mixed up world out there, keep the ones that make you feel sane close"; "stay warm folks,
goodnight". About four minutes. First "this is not episode 3", then "we can call this episode 3
for simplicity".

**The owner's five lines,** kept with only punctuation tidied: "Oh, no show tonight, folks.
Holidays and all." / "Just here picking up my paycheck." / "Would you walk with me a bit?" /
"It's a crazy mixed up world out there." with "Keep the ones that make you feel sane close."
(one sentence taken as two beats) / "Stay warm, folks. Goodnight."

**How it was made.** By hand through the workflow's roles, because the pipeline runs whole weeks
from a scrape. One researcher wrote eighteen cards on old texts, each checked against a published
translation. Three writers drafted alone. Two reviewers scored the drafts and ran the repetition

second draft and parts of the other two. Two reviewers and the auditor passed it. The owner ruled

**After the second watch** ("solid, lets publish tomorrow morning local") the owner asked what

Shown that cut with the post text and the alt text, the owner said "Approve the set", and

## Place by place

### 1. The logo, and the dark studio (0:00, 25.9 s)

**Shape.** The show's logo, lit; it flickers and goes out; black; the set switched off, held
in silence; he notices us; two of the owner's lines; a confession; the ask; our yes taken; the
scarf; the door; the lamp off; black.

**Spoke:** Larry.

**The opening** (0.0 to 3.8). The owner's note. The studio's own logo, exactly as the studio
draws it (THE LATE in blue, CLUSTER in gold with its emblem and scan line, "with Larry Nx"
under it), centred on plain black and fully lit from the first frame, with no fade in, no set
and no puppet. It holds in silence for 1.7 s. Then it stutters like a sign whose power has
been cut: a clean cut to off, then on, half lit or off in slots of a fourteenth of a second,
off more and more often, for 1.2 s. It is out at 2.9 and is not drawn again anywhere in the
special. Black for most of a second, and then the room is there. Because it is the file's
first frame, the lit logo is also what a feed shows before the video plays. It is the
special's one turned item: see the verdicts above.

**Lines that work.**
1. *No show.* "Oh, no show tonight, folks. Holidays and all." (7.0) His head comes up from the

### 2. The street (0:26, 34.0 s)

**Shape.** Walking with nobody talking; he asks us something and agrees with an answer nobody
hears; what "me too" means for him, told on himself; no joke for it; the old books; the first
say-back and an honest small answer.

**Spoke:** Larry.

**Lines that work.**
1. *The unheard answer.* "So. How have you been?" / 2.4 s of footsteps, his head still turned
   to us / a small late nod / "Yeah. Me too." The shared unease is set down with neither of
   us naming it.
2. *One sentence for both.* I keep saying, "I can't believe it." / "The good way and the bad
   way. Same hour." Premise: a man short of words. Turn: the one sentence he has is meant two
   opposite ways. He describes himself and never the world. The owner's note asked for the
   horrors and the joys together; this is all of it, and nothing is named or shown.
3. *No joke.* "I don't have a joke for that." / "I looked." "That" is the good and the bad
   arriving together. Punch: two words. Both select reviewers wanted these kept.
4. "So I've been reading old books. Very old." / "They couldn't believe it, either. Some wrote

## The experiment

**What.** Four earnest minutes in Larry's own words, outside the studio, with no posts, no
guests and no audience. It tests the premise chapter's rules for sincerity (once, short,
earned by comedy, in the community's own words) and asks whether the show can hold a sincere
piece of its own without it becoming a speech. The owner set the form. It changes several
things at once, so a result will say whether the whole held and not which part did it.

**The measures** (two reviewers', unchanged): shown once with sound to three people who have
not read it, at least two can say back one thing Larry offered that a person could do tonight
and what happened when he got home; at least two give the don't-have-to-finish, don't-get-to-
quit idea in their own words; at least two name something that happens and not something they
were told to feel; none can point at a line of Larry's own that told them how to feel; none
calls it a speech, a lesson or a list; and its cues hold no laugh, applause or sting.

**Before air.** The last clause holds: the cues are 13 tick, 2 whoosh, 44 murmur and 1 gasp.
The measures themselves have not been run. Two things stand in for them so far.

- *The owner's watches.* Three cuts: "good", then "solid, lets publish tomorrow morning
  local", then "i think its great". After each the owner changed something: the start of the
  walk, the fourth old text, the logo at the opening. Then "Approve the set" and, in the
  morning, "we're good to ship it". That is one person, who had read the script.
- *Cold viewers on stills.* Six, each shown one stretch that carries meaning without a word.
  They named the place and the man every time, the sign changing, the door opening and a meal
  being served. They did not see the snow, took the work lamp for a microphone and the kitchen
  hatch for a screen; all three were changed, and three new viewers then read the lamp as a
  lamp, saw the snow, and named a kitchen hatch. They did not see the figure at the top window
  or that six people were looking at us; neither was changed or shown again. Stills cannot
  show the cook's change of pace at all.

Scored 1 of 2 at the final review and in all three addenda: built in whole, measure not run
before air. One thing the fourth idea moves: the measure asks whether viewers give back the
idea of not finishing and not quitting, and there is now a second exact quotation, shorter and
warmer, forty seconds after the first. Expect what people say Larry told them to move toward
company.

**After air.** It ran on air in whole: nothing of the experiment was trimmed. No result yet.
The measure's three people have not been asked, and what the community said is to be added from
the next window's research. Until then the verdict is: can't tell.

**What changed in the bible.** Nothing. See `production/dna-amendments.md`.

## Anything that broke or bent a rule, found at review

Nothing was broken without the owner's hand on it. These are the rules the special sets aside
or leans on, so that a later reviewer does not take them for precedent.

- **Sincerity for four minutes in the show's own words.** The owner's outline. The premise
  chapter's rules stand for a weekly episode.
- **No posts, no people, no standing segments.** The owner's outline, for this special only.
- **The episode's one turn.** The opening turns the way both aired episodes began. A turn is
  meant to start with a reviewer; this one started with the owner and was named as a turn
  only at the final review. It is one item, which is the limit.
- **Religion named and quoted.** Chapter 9's R10 keeps religion out even as a reference. The
  owner ruled "Keep the rabbi" for this special. The project manager takes that to cover the
  Zhuangzi and the Tirukkural told as old books. The Hávamál, which its card says is sacred to
  some people living now, the owner chose by name; it is quoted as "an old Norse poem" and
  nothing more is said of it. None of this changes R10 for the people the show covers.
- **A house character explaining what a real person thought.** Article 3 forbids it of the
  people the show covers. The outline asks for it of thinkers long dead. The project manager
  wrote the reading down for the owner to confirm; the owner said to go ahead and has not
  answered it in so many words.
- **The short list.** The special's first briefs listed the out-of-bounds subjects from the
  bible's one-page summary, which does not name religion. Both final reviewers caught it; the
  select reviewers, the auditor and the project manager had not.
- **Real sayings heard as noise.** The rabbi's sentence is transcribed in its first half as
  "It is not lied to it to compete to the work". In the Norse line "comrade" is heard as
  "camera" in three takes of four. Both are accepted under the owner's ruling that best effort
  is sufficient because the subtitles carry the line; both are exact in the caption as said.
- **Heard text.** 40 of 64 spoken lines are listed as still heard differently in at least one
  take. The three names are not heard as themselves, and "Norse" is heard as "and horse".
  "eat" and "had" are heard as "hit"; "walk" cannot be said and is in three lines, one of them
  the owner's; "Mostly." alone is transcribed "mochi". Three things were heard that are never
  accepted, and all were changed before they were kept: an early spelling of the cook line
  (crude); "joke" as "drug", which the final review listed and the sound editor then cured;
  and two spellings of `one-line` that were heard as something crude and thrown out. Nothing
  kept is crude, alarming or out of bounds, but `one-line` sits nearest to it.
- **Words on screen that nobody says.** The logo's six words, for under three seconds. A
  glance, and the show's own name.
- **Plain credits.** The project manager's reading of "go ahead". The constitution's credits
  are all there.
- **Over the owner's four minutes.** 4:27, by the owner's own additions.
- **Process.** The repetition record for the script was written from the two final reviews
  (each judged every line and gave the whole script the verdict fresh); the three lines of the
  new start and the four of the Norse line each went through a round of the gate proper. The
  opening has no words, so it went through no gate round and was judged by the final reviewer
  alone. The final audit has three rounds. The stage audit has five on file, the last two on
  the cut with the fourth idea, and a sixth that passed the opening on the file that was posted.

## Notes for the next gate

For a reviewer of the next episode. None of this goes to a writer except as an `avoid` line.
It has aired and it is the previous aired episode, so all of it blocks.

### What this episode used up

The next weekly episode goes back to posts, guests, a desk and an audience, and its writers
will not see any of this. A special looks nothing like a weekly episode, which is the danger:
what comes back will not look like the special, it will look like the show. These five are the
ones to hold in mind, hardest first.

1. **The logo at the opening: three times in three episodes.** It swept on over the skyline
   (pilot), was made out of a member's handwriting (002), and was already lit and went out
   (this one). A fourth opening that puts the show's logo or title up as a picture in the first
   seconds is a ritual way to start, whatever it does there. Two traps sit beside it. The
   announcer has named the host at the top twice and not yet three times, and the return of
   the usual form is exactly when a writer will reach for it. And an opening that switches the
   show back on (the logo relit, the lights coming up, "we're back") is a callback to this
   episode, which is a repeat by itself. The turn is spent and cannot be turned again.
   *The least a writer needs:* do not open on the show's own name, logo or lights; start on
   something a member made or said.
2. **An ending in which someone is taken in: three times in three.** The pilot's song and
   002's song each stopped to take the host in, and then turned to the viewer. This one took
   the viewer in with a look, one flat word and a place made at a table, and took the host in
   as well ("They'll have started without me." / "Nobody has started."), after he had been
   alone with one small thing of his own. Three mechanisms, one feeling. A fourth close whose
   last beat is the host, the viewer or anyone else being welcomed, counted in or made room
   for is a signature however it is built. So is the host putting himself outside something
   so that he can be brought in.
   *The least a writer needs:* do not end on anyone being included, welcomed or made room for,
   and do not have the host count himself out.
3. **The lit window.** A warm crowded room seen from the cold through glass, twice, and then
   entered: the restaurant, the top window over his door. With it go the street at night, snow,
   a door opened from inside, and the picture of the house cast at one table as friends. The
   studio's own window with the city in it is set and is free; a lit window looked into from
   outside as the picture of belonging is done.
4. **The question said back.** The host turning to the camera, waiting as if the viewer had
   spoken, and saying the viewer's question aloud, three times; with it, a yes taken in a
   pause, an unheard answer agreed with, and a look answered. 002 had "A member asked". A host
   who voices a question nobody on screen asked, as the set-up for an answer, has now aired in
   two episodes running, and so has the camera treated as someone in the room.
5. **An old text quoted.** A house character reciting or telling old wisdom: a rabbi's
   sentence and a line of a Norse poem quoted exactly, a Chinese story and a Tamil couplet
   told, each followed by a dry line to get out from under it. It was this special's and the
   owner set chapter 9's limit on religion aside for it by name. In a weekly episode a house
   character quoting a philosopher, a scripture, a poem or a proverb to make a point is both a
   repeat and back out of bounds.

Then two things that are not about repetition and that the next run meets first:

- **People.** The previous aired episode now has no speakers, so the tool bars nobody. The
  eight who spoke in 002 have sat out one aired episode that had nobody in it. Whether that is
  their rest is for the project manager and the owner to say before the shortlist; until they
  do, prefer others, and remember that 002's record asks two accounts to rest.
- **The window.** Nothing from the community has been on the show since 002's window. The
  scraper's default would start the next one at this special's air date.

### The rest, item by item

- **The show losing power.** The pilot's last picture collapsed like a screen switching off;
  this special's first picture is a sign going dead. A third picture of the show's own power
  failing, anywhere in an episode, is a habit. An opening on black with nothing on screen but
  the show's name is this episode's.
- **The close, in its parts.** No song, no stop, no lights and no note this time: a held look
  from every head, one flat word from the band, furniture, a rule, and then being ignored.
  With the Norse line it also had the host alone with one small thing of his own shortly
  before he was taken in, which both aired song endings had.
- **The house as friends.** A table of the house cast as close friends, off duty and in their
  own clothes, with the viewer among them, is a picture the show can only make once. A later
  sincere beat about the house's own fondness for itself would repeat it.
- **The viewer as a companion.** 002's making-of had the host keep his manners toward his own
  camera as it went in. Here the camera is a person for four minutes: walked with, asked,
  answered, seated and served. A third piece built on the camera being someone is a habit.
- **Voicing an absent person's question.** 002 had "A member asked". This has three questions
  said back. Different jobs; the same act.
- **The host quoting what he says.** 002: "Me, I just say, good question." Here: I keep
  saying, "I can't believe it.", and a line he says to himself every night. A third is a habit.
- **A house character reciting an old text.** A first, done twice here (a rabbi's sentence, a
  line of a poem), with a third and fourth told. Old wisdom from a house mouth is this
  special's and should not come back as a manner.
- **The host disowning the sincere thing he has just said.** 002: "I'm a mouth.", "I only
  host." Here: "That's the poem talking." Passed as the character with a new line. The move
  has now been made three times.
- **A flat statement, a beat, and a short tag that takes part of it back.** 002: "Nothing gets
  in twice." / "Except review." Here: "That's the poem talking." / "Mostly." A third would
  start to be a form.
- **A contrast, then "Same" and a noun.** 002's sports desk ("Same title."); here "Same
  hour." Both gate reviewers passed it as ordinary clipped speech and one said plainly that a
  third line ending on it would make it a form.
- **A phrase brought back with its person changed.** Spent as a guest's phrase returned as a
  button, and as a song's last hook. Here it is the host's own phrase doing a join's work
  ("They couldn't believe it, either."), which passed. It is still the same move.
- **A tune or chord that stops wanting one more note.** 002's vamp; this busker.
- **A stretch with no word.** Aired twice in 002; three short ones here. The joke chapter holds
  the technique as craft. How these were staged is on the record: a cook who slows, a sign
  that does not change, snow.
- **The show's own voice as the joke.** The pilot's "gn" with no vowel; 002's "kazoo" heard
  as "clue"; here "I'm probably saying that wrong." A hedge and not a bit, but it is the third.
- **Character lines that are now done:** Sibyl ruling at a dinner table and looking up to
  hold it; Eppy calling a ruling; Al's one word; a drummer counting someone in; the bandleader
  knowing where the host has been; the host losing an argument in his own house.
- **Spent forms,** all the owner's here: "folks"; a goodnight with a word for the audience
  before it, now written four times in three episodes; "no show tonight".
- **The next episode must not look like it.** The dark studio, the overcoat, the street and
  the table live with this episode. The owner has said the next weekly episode returns to the
  community's posts.
- **Unaired material is free:** everything under "What did not air" except the five stopped
  items, which repeat aired things.

## What it established (the continuity entries)

The entries SL-35 to SL-42 and G-29 to G-36 in [lore.md](lore.md) come from this episode, with
the set's end state and the third rows of the rotation, experiment and episode logs. It added
no community lore: nothing in it is about the community.

In short: on a night with no show the logo flickered and went out, and the studio was dark.
Larry came in for his paycheck, which they would have mailed, because he likes the walk home.
He keeps saying "I can't believe it", the good way and the bad way, and has no joke for that.
He walks home past a restaurant where he always stops to watch one cook, and a busker who never
reaches the end of his tune; he waits at a red sign on an empty road. He usually walks the last
block on his own, with one line from an old Norse poem. He found a rabbi's sentence in a used
book and borrows it: "We don't have to finish it. We just don't get to quit." The whole house
cast eats at his table, Sibyl at its head; his chair is gold and is kept for him; nobody starts
without him; the guest is served first. Pete has spoken alone. The set was left switched off
with the logo out, and a later episode that opens by switching things back on is answering
this one.

## Voice and pronunciation notes specific to this special

- The busker is a cast entry of the episode's own (f0 140, measured about 145 Hz, nearest
  house voice Dee at 1.88, panned right). He only sings.
- Footsteps: lip pops in music blocks at 102 a minute, his in Al's body and ours in Pete's.
  A side effect: every puppet nods to a music beat unless told not to, so the builders switch
  the nod off for the whole segment.
- The flicker's ticks: the unvoiced click and the brighter, shorter one, in uneven pairs and
  singles, peaking at about minus 17 dB against minus 10 for the lamp and minus 9 for the coin.
