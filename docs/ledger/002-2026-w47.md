## The experiment

**What.** In each segment drafted from a made thing, the standing three aside, one stretch of at
least ten seconds with no spoken or sung word and no crowd sound, in which the thing is shown in
use and the segment's turn happens as something the thing does. It tested the joke chapter's "the
voice says the setup and the punch".

**Was it run.** Partly. Three stretches were built (plotter 12.50 s, coffee 13.15 s, chess
11.87 s). Two aired. The plotter's was cut in the owner's trim: "i felt too much time was given
to the plotter, 300/300 is not that interesting."

**Did it work.** For the two that aired, by the proxy that was run: three cold viewers, each given
one frame a second of one stretch, named the writer's turn for the coffee segment ("the kettle
tilts to refill the cup") and for chess ("'Larry's side' has no separate side from anyone else's"),
and not for the plotter (the pen's row was read as a subtitle running off the edge). The limits
are large: the viewers were a small model, on stills, with no sound. One person has since watched
cold, the owner, who kept the two the viewers could name and cut the one they could not.

**What the three had in common, and not.** In the two that worked, the turn is a change of state
that can be watched: an empty mug is filled; a board turns one colour. In the one that did not,
the turn had to be read off a row of small letters and rested on a fact (the character limit)
that was only told afterwards, and the lines after the stretch did the explaining.

**What changed in the bible.** `06-jokes.md`: a thing shown working may take the punch itself with
no word said, when the turn is something it visibly does; once in an episode. So the technique is
craft and a writer may arrive at it again. What is done, as devices, is how these two were
staged (the house at the edges watching; the host looking round for an opponent; the refill; the
lap).

**What it cost.** In the six-segment draft, three of six segments shared one inner shape. In what
aired, two of nine. The chess stretch shows a property of the board, not the game in play, because
nobody had seen the game.

**After air.** To be added from the next window's research.
