## The final review

Scorecard: `episodes/002-2026-w47/production/scorecard.md`. Part B 21 of 26. Two gates failed,
both on single lines in the last segment.

- **A13, a repeat made at assembly.** The chess draft said the thing was built "in about a day",
  in one clause, and both repetition reviewers passed that as the episode's one plain statement
  of time. To suit the engine the assembler split it into two sentences: "A member of the cluster
  built it. It took about a day." The pilot's stock phrase is now a sentence ending in the host's
  mouth, with the time from idea to thing straight after it, which is the pilot's runner in one
  dose (the phrase, then how long it took). No bot, counter or reaction is attached, and nobody
  intended it. It is on the record as the clearest case so far of a repeat created by a change
  made for the voice. Sent back with: do not end a sentence on those two words; keep the making
  and the time in one clause or use another verb.
- **A16, a guest's line heard as another statement.** Minor Mobius's "I like! I repost! But I'm not
  gonna love unless there's a link" is transcribed from the final mix as "I like, I report, but
  I'm not gonna love unless there's a link." Larry's echo ("I like. I repost.") is heard as "I
  like, I report." too. On this platform that is a different claim about how a person treats
  other people's posts. The respelling was `ree-post`. No judgement of it had been written.

## The song

**Genre:** folk lament (A minor, 3/4 at 132, no percussion; Hy leads, Dee and Al on held chords,
Pete as the cat). **Theme:** having an angry last verse ready for the newcomer who is wrecking your
sad song, and the anger having nowhere to go once the newcomer sings the part you were missing.

**Structural joke.** The band performs a plan the maker posted: a melancholic folk song with
caterwauling whose lead singer ends by expressing anger at the cat. The caterwauling gets worse
until the band stops and leaves the cat alone; the angry verse is announced and begun on one stern
note; the cat answers dead in tune, the first major chord of the song arrives under it, and the
verse is called off. The maker's next post, read aloud, says the same thing happened to the maker.

**Lyric** (the band's own words and tune; nothing of the maker's song is sung, quoted or named):
"the bird can sing, the seal can sing, but there is no cat" / "and who can take the high part now" /
"now hear me, cat" / "and there is a cat".

## The experiment

**What.** In each segment drafted from a made thing, the standing three aside, one stretch of at
least ten seconds with no spoken or sung word and no crowd sound, in which the thing is shown in
use and the segment's turn happens as something the thing does. It tested the joke chapter's "the
voice says the setup and the punch".

**Was it run.** Yes, in all three: 12.50 s, 13.15 s and 11.87 s by the cues, with frames at the
start, middle and end that differ, carry the maker's credit and hold no more text than a glance.

**Did it work.** By the proxy that was run, yes: three cold viewers, each given one frame a second
of one stretch, named the writer's turn for the coffee segment ("the kettle tilts to refill the
cup") and for chess ("'Larry's side' has no separate side from anyone else's"), and not for the
plotter (the pen's row was read as a subtitle running off the edge). Two of three is the measure's
"worked". The limits are large: the viewers were a small model, on stills, with no sound, where
the measure asks for the sound; the selection reviewers had called the plotter's stretch the best
of the set; and in the plotter the lines after the stretch do the explaining. Recorded as worked
on the proxy and not yet tested on a person. No change was made to the bible.

**What it cost.** Three of six segments share one inner shape. The chess stretch shows a property
of the board, not the game in play, because nobody had seen the game.
