# Terms of use: the "making of" card

For the card `production/research/cards/making-of.select.json` and its fact sheet
`production/research/production-facts.md`. Set by an auditor on 21 November 2026. These terms
apply to every brief, idea and script that uses the card. The constitution
(`docs/bible/00-constitution.md`, `docs/bible/09-real-people.md`) applies in full; nothing here
relaxes it.

**Pronouns.** Use a pronoun for a real person only where `production/research/pronouns.md`
records one, and then that one; where none is stated (the owner has stated none), use the name,
the handle or "they". Never infer one.

## What the commission lifts, and what it does not

- For this script only, the bible's limit of one joke about the show itself does not apply: the
  show is the subject, by the owner's choice. The craft rule that the show is not its own subject
  is lifted with it, for this script.
- Nothing in the constitution is lifted. Every real person on the card says only what they
  published, in its context; nothing invented is attributed to anyone; the out-of-bounds subjects
  stay out.

Nobody on the card spoke in the aired episode, so the people rule does not bar any of them. If a
script wants anyone else who is a real account, it needs a card of its own; posts about the show
not on this card are not available.

**Occasions.** The owner's three launch-thread posts were said announcing the show, crediting it,
and pointing at its source, one after another in the owner's own thread, on the night it launched,
with nobody prompting them; a staging recreates that occasion (the owner announcing and crediting
the owner's own show, unprompted), or the posts appear as the cards they were, dated 13 November.
The owner's question and follow-up were said six days later, replying to a
community member's post about the show; they are not answers to anything a house character asks
about the episode's content. Nothing on the card says what the owner did after asking, and the card
records no answer to those replies. The one decision on record since is the owner's ruling
of 21 November on tagging, which the fact sheet states; a script may use it only as "The turn to
the audience" (below) allows. Beyond that, a script may not say or imply that the owner took any
suggestion, decided anything, or replied.

## What on the card may not be used

- The launch post's video, and anything it shows: it is the aired episode. Only the post's text.
- The launch thread's second post and every other post about the show that is not on the card.
- The post nate replies under, and anything about his work.
- Anything that implies the show sends direct messages, interviews people, or runs a producer bot
  because of these replies. The suggestions may be shown as what they were: suggestions.
- From the fact sheet: the two mishearings that touched out-of-bounds subjects. The sheet does not
  say what they were, and a script may not guess, quote or allude to them (R10). The harmless
  mishearings listed by name may be used as facts about the transcription check, as the sheet
  states them; the words that were misheard came from real people's posts, so a script names the
  word and what was heard, and never says or shows whose post it was or quotes more of it.
- From the fact sheet: nothing about the unaired draft's segments beyond what the sheet says. Its
  facts are about the process, and no script may reconstruct what that draft's segments were
  about, or the earlier aired episode's, from them.
- No invented process facts. A number, a stage, a role, a count of tokens, a time or an incident
  is used only as the fact sheet states it; anything else in a script about how the show is made
  is visibly the show's own fiction (R5, R8), never something a viewer could take for a record.
  The recursion may be invented as staging (a room inside a room), but each level's facts are the
  sheet's.

## The turn to the audience

Added on 21 November 2026 for the owner's addition to the commission (`production/commission.md`)
and the fact sheet's section "Who makes the show, and how to point it at something". Every
making-of script must have this turn; the wording is the writer's and is Larry's.

**Who says it, and to whom.** Larry says it, in the show's own voice, as the show's standing
invitation. No puppet of a real account says it, and it is never put in the owner's mouth or on a
card of the owner's. It is addressed to the community the show is about: the words make that
plain at least once (the cluster, the simcluster, the people this show is about, or the like), so
that every "you" in the invitation is the community. It never says, and never implies by a
picture, that a tag from anyone at all, whoever is watching, will be looked at.

**What it may say.**
- That they make this show, in the sense the sheet gives: every episode starts from what the
  community posted that week, every joke rests on a real thing from those posts, and a puppet of a
  real account says only what that account published.
- That tagging the show's account guides where the show looks, from now on. The handle, if shown
  or said, is exactly `@latecluster.simcluster.tv`.
- That a tag is a look and nothing more: what is tagged passes the same screen, gates and reviewers
  as everything else, most of what any week turns up is not used, and the show does not reply to
  tags. That several members pointing at one thing add up and one member pointing many times does
  not, as the sheet says.
- That the owner ruled it on 21 November, only as a process fact in the sheet's terms, said by a
  house character, and never as a reply to the owner's question thread or to anyone's suggestion.

**What it may never say or imply.**
- A promise of a place on the show, of being used, of a puppet, of a reply, of being tagged back
  or mentioned by the account, or of a direct message. "If" is the most it offers.
- That a tag from anyone at all is read, or that an account outside the community is looked at.
  No "anyone", "everyone", "whoever's watching" attached to the tag.
- That tagging more often counts for more, or any weight for any particular person.
- What is in the lane "what the audience pointed at", that anything has been pointed at yet, or
  that anything on the show came from a tag.
- That the audience writes, chooses, judges or approves the show, or that anyone's post will be
  used without passing the rules.
- An invitation to send direct messages or to point at anything private.

**The suggestion on the card, beside it.** bisks' "a bot that people tag" may stand in the same
segment as the invitation, and may hang beside it, as what it was: a dated card, a reply to the
owner's follow-up, with that follow-up shown or read just before it (as above). A house line may
say about it only what the sheet says: that members of the community had asked for something like
this in public. Nothing in words or picture says or implies that the owner took it, that the
invitation exists because of it, that it is answered, credited or thanked for, or that the show is
now the bot it describes. The owner's question and follow-up may hang in the same segment, but the
invitation is never staged as their answer: no line, cut or picture presents it as what came of
them. bisks' second suggestion (the producer bot) does not stand beside the invitation at all.

## The AI models and the company that makes them

- The show may describe what its own records establish: that its agents run on Claude Fable,
  Claude Opus and Claude Haiku, in the roles the sheet lists; the owner's public credit line, as
  the card it is; the measured token use, called "effective tokens" as the sheet defines them; the
  owner's ruling on what Fable is for; and what happened in the run, as the sheet states it.
- It asserts nothing else about the models or about Anthropic: no claim about what a model
  thinks, wants, feels or intends; nothing about how a model works inside, how it compares with
  others, what it costs in money, or what it will do next; nothing about the company's plans,
  products, motives or people; no invented quote from a model or the company. A model is never a
  speaking character presented as the real model saying something; a house character may be a
  role in the process (a writer, a reviewer) when it is plainly the show's own puppet.
- A joke may point at the process, the show, its tools and its rules, and at power in general
  (R6), but it may not present an invented fact about the models or the company as true.

## Required on screen

The parody label on any puppet of a real account, the disclaimer at the first one, and the
credits naming the real accounts whose personas were played and saying every voice is
synthesized (Article 4, R9). Every real post shown is exact, with its date, display name, handle
and like count, and cut only with an ellipsis that keeps its meaning (R4).
