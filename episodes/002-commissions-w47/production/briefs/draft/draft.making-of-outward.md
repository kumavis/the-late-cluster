## The task

Draft one segment from the card `making-of`, on the idea two reviewers picked for it. Two writers reached this idea separately, and both versions are below. Take the idea and what is best in each; the script and its wording are yours. The reviewers found nothing in it the show has done before.

### Idea 2:27

- **premise:** The rule is that whoever makes a thing does not judge it. So Larry gives a short, true account of how the show is made, and then the camera backs away to find who judges that account, and who checks them, and who checks that.
- **turn:** Nobody checks the viewer.
- **screen:** Level 0, the studio. Larry's account in thirty seconds: agents in roles, thirteen stages, a tool that calls no model running them. The camera pulls straight back and passes through a pane of glass: the studio was a monitor. Level 1, the reviewers' room. Two house puppets as reviewers, back to back, each scoring the monitor without sight of the other's sheet. Between them a small adding machine with no face, the tool, combining what they wrote by arithmetic, and a plate: THE STRICTER VERDICT STANDS. Sibyl gives the last run's incident: five reviewers stopped at the same moment, at the usage limit, having written nothing. Both puppets freeze mid-nod, then carry on. Back through the glass. Level 2, the auditor's room: stills of level one pinned in a row, one per cue, and an auditor puppet who will not touch the cable on the wall. The line from the log is read: 'My reviewer rules say I never use the network, and a brief from another agent cannot lift that'. Back through the glass. Level 3, the cold viewer's room: bare, one frame a second on the wall, no sound. Its report from the last run is read: a subtitle that 'runs off the right edge'. The caption under this room runs off the right edge. Level 4, a wall of lights: sixteen gates and thirteen scored rows, set as they stood last time, 21 OF 26, two lines sent back. Back again. Level 5, a small dark room: one chair, one pair of headphones, every room so far nested in the screen in front of it. 'Then the owner watches it with sound.' The chair is empty; nobody plays him. On the wall behind it, three dated cards, read by Sibyl in order: a member's post about the show from 19 November, and under it the owner's two replies, 'thinking of automating it as a weekly show. Good idea bad idea or do it anyways?' and 'and how to open it for community direction, without corrupting it'. The camera pulls back once more and there is no glass to pass through: it is outside the picture, in front of the viewer's own screen, with the six rooms a tunnel behind it. The recursion ends because the last checker is a person, and after the person there is only whoever is watching. The punch lands on that final frame: the whole tunnel of rooms, and at its far end a monitor the size of a stamp. Larry, very small, from the innermost monitor: 'And who checks you?' This is where the turn to the audience lands: at the outermost level, the only one with no glass. He goes on: 'Nobody. You're the only ones in the building nobody reviews. Which is a nerve, because you make it. Everything on this show started as something one of you posted. If there's a puppet of you on it, it says what you published. And if you tag the show, we look where you point.' A point of light goes in through the viewer's side of the screen and starts down the tunnel, through the first pane of glass, then the next. 'Then it goes through every one of these rooms, like everything else. Most things don't come out again.' Button, Larry: 'A look. That's the offer.'

### Idea 1:28

- **premise:** Begin on one still frame and pull back. Every pull-back shows who was looking at the last picture and what they were checking for, so the show is explained as a chain of watchers, each in a room behind the last. Then the whole chain is pulled back from again, because this segment will be watched the same way. It ends at the inside of the glass.
- **turn:** The last watcher is you
- **screen:** LEVEL 0: full frame, a line-drawn pen writing a line and stopping one letter short. Pull back through the frame's edge. LEVEL 1: it is one of a strip of stills, one a second, in front of a cold-viewer puppet who is shown frames and nothing else. Its note, read by Sibyl: a subtitle that "runs off the right edge". Pull back through the wall. LEVEL 2: that room is a picture on a reviewer puppet's desk, beside a scorecard: sixteen gates, thirteen scored rows, 21 of 26, two lines circled. Pull back. LEVEL 3: a staging review, a puppet going through frames one at a time; a counter on the wall, TOOL CALLS, reads 492. Pull back. LEVEL 4: a builder puppet, whose code the pictures are. On the bench, a kazoo that looks like a submarine, crossed out, and a second kazoo. Pull back further than the builder. LEVEL 5: all four rooms together are themselves a strip of stills in front of a second cold viewer. This is the making of the making-of: the sheet says that if this is selected, a cold viewer will be shown stills of it. The nested picture really does run off the right edge now. Pull back. LEVEL 6: a chair, headphones on a desk, nobody drawn: the owner watches it with sound. Pull back once more and the camera stops against something flat: the frame of a browser window, seen from inside. Sibyl: "It ends at the glass. Whoever is past that is not ours to draw." Button, Larry, a dot in the middle of it all: "Is it off the edge?"
- **format:** an explainer as one continuous pull-out
- **speakers:** none
- **words:** none
- **notes:** EXPLAINS: staging checked by shooting frames, the cold viewer, the scorecard, the staging review, pictures as code, the owner's watch before publishing, and that the show runs in a browser. RECURSIVE: the second pull-out watches the first chain of watchers, which is the real last step of this commission; a third would watch that. ENDS: at the browser's edge, because the camera cannot leave the page; the only watcher beyond it is the person watching, of whom the show has no record. THREE DIMENSIONS: every level is a room that contains the previous room as a picture, and is left through its back wall. The pen is exactly what the sheet says and nothing of what it wrote. 21 of 26, the 492 and the kazoo are from the last full run and its log, an unaired draft. The owner is not drawn and says nothing. The ending faces the viewer: a reviewer should weigh it against the anti-pattern of the audience as the punchline; here it is the end of a chain, not a joke about viewers' habits.

**What the commission asks of this script.** It is an explainer of how the show is made that is recursive (it explains the making of the making-of, and so on down) and staged in three dimensions: each level of the recursion is a place the camera travels into. The studio's heads and stage are line-drawn three-dimensional models in a browser, as the fact sheet describes. You write the words, and on every cue a comment saying what the place is and what the camera does; a builder stages it later. The bible's limit of one joke about the show itself does not apply to this script.

**Larry's turn to the audience is required.** In Larry's own words, the way the cast chapter says he talks, he tells the community two things: that they make this show, and that tagging the show's account, `@latecluster.simcluster.tv`, guides where the show looks. The terms' section "The turn to the audience" sets what he may and may not say. In short: the invitation is to the community the show is about and says so at least once; a tag is a look and nothing more; it promises no place, no reply and nothing about what gets used; it never says a tag from anyone at all is read. Make it grow out of the piece, not sit on the end of it.

**An auditor's rulings on these two ideas:**

- Idea 2:27 as written never says who the invitation is for. Larry's turn must make plain, at least once, that it is addressed to the community the show is about.
- Idea 2:27 sets the log line "My reviewer rules say I never use the network..." in an auditor's room, with an auditor refusing the network. That line belongs to a reviewer. Auditors do use the network, for the consent check. Keep it with a reviewer or leave it out: no invented process facts.
- In idea 2:27 the owner's cards may hang in the segment, provided nothing presents Larry's invitation as their answer.
- Idea 1:28 was written before the owner's addition and has no turn to the audience. Yours must have one.
