## The task

Draft one segment from the card `making-of`, on the idea two reviewers picked for it. Two writers reached this idea separately, and both versions are below. Take the idea and what is best in each; the script and its wording are yours. The reviewers found nothing in it the show has done before.

### Idea 2:25

- **premise:** Larry says one sentence, 'This is how the show is made', and the camera goes into his mouth to find out how the sentence was made. Each level down is a throat, and the smaller Larry at the bottom of each throat explains how the level above came to exist: first the show, then this explainer of the show, then the fact sheet the explainer was written from.
- **turn:** The bottom voice is borrowed.
- **screen:** Level 0, the studio. Larry says that every voice here is synthesized from a model of a vocal tract, and that the mouths are drawn working because the heads move exactly as the engine's did. On 'mouths' the camera flies in past his lips, tongue and velum, each named in one glance-sized label. Level 1, his throat, drawn as a tunnel of thirteen glowing rings: the thirteen stages. The camera passes through them while a small Larry at the far end names them at speed, with one real thing hung on a ring as it goes by (ring four: three booths with their backs to each other, and 548; ring eight: WRITTEN kazoo, HEARD clue). He says how his own line got here: rendered offline, its timing written to a cue file, the picture code timed by that file; the rings pulse on his word timings. Into his mouth. Level 2, a shorter throat of seven rings: the seven steps of this commission. The owner asked; the manager wrote a fact sheet; an auditor checked every line; writers brainstormed without seeing each other's; reviewers; a draft. Ring six is the one the camera is inside; ring seven is dark. A smaller Larry: 'Whoever wrote this was standing here.' Into his mouth. Level 3, a few rings only: the fact sheet being made. Written; checked line by line; lines taken out for want of a record, then put back from the log in the log's own words. A very small Larry reads one of the restored lines: 'The first kazoo looked like a submarine; I redrew it.' Into his mouth. Bottom: no rings and no Larry. One bare vocal tract turning slowly in the dark, jaw, tongue, lips, velum, with a credit, CHIMERA · jamesondh · open source, and beside it the owner's card from 13 November, its second line read by Sibyl: 'chimera voice synth and head model from', and the link. The recursion ends here because this is the one layer the show did not make, and it cannot give the making-of of something it did not make. The punch lands on the bare vocal tract with the credit under it. Here, at the bottom, is the turn to the audience: the empty mouth swings round to face the lens and Larry's voice comes out of it. 'That's the bottom. A voice somebody lent us. And nothing to say with it until one of you posts. Everything up there started with something you put online. If we build a puppet of you, it says what you published and not a word more. So you make this show. I open and shut. And if you tag the show, we look where you point. That's all a tag does. We look. I promise nothing. I'm a mouth.' The account's handle is on screen for a glance as he says it. Then the credit scrolls up, more credits follow, and the camera rides the end roll back out through all four mouths. Button, full-size Larry, closing his mouth: 'Excuse me.'

- **speakers:** none
- **words:** at://did:plc:c2x5i7fneh7xbvpvw2r3omes/app.bsky.feed.post/3n25vm7gzms23
- **notes:** Answers all three parts. It explains what the show is made of and its stages (levels 0 and 1); the making of this making-of (level 2, the commission's seven steps); the making of that account (level 3, the sheet's own header and last section); and it ends at the borrowed voice. Thirteen and seven are the sheet's numbers. The number of rings at level 3 is my grouping of what the sheet says about itself, not a number it gives: draw them, and do not say a count. Every incident is the sheet's. jamesondh is credited as the sheet and the owner's post credit him, and nothing else is said about him. The owner's post is a card read by a house character, so there is no puppet of the owner. No model speaks: every Larry is the house puppet, and the nested Larrys say nothing that is new fact. Each level is a place the camera enters through a mouth; a builder nests four scenes. The owner's addition lands at the bottom level, spoken by Larry out of the bare vocal tract: the borrowed voice has nothing to say until the community posts. His wording is a draft; it offers a look and nothing more, as the sheet has it (a tag guides attention and decides nothing else, and the show does not reply to tags).

### Idea 1:25

- **premise:** Larry begins, "This is how the show is made," and the camera stops listening and goes into his mouth to see for itself. Each place it reaches is the making of the place before, and the way down is always through whatever is doing the talking. It ends when the head it is trying to enter is forty-two pixels across.
- **turn:** The bottom is a profile picture
- **screen:** LEVEL 0, the desk: Larry starts the sentence. The camera pushes between his lips. LEVEL 1, inside the head: the vocal tract as a cave in glowing lines, the jaw a drawbridge, the tongue a moving floor, the lips the gate behind us, the velum a curtain. Larry, voice over: every voice is synthesized from this model, and the heads move exactly as the engine's did. The owner's post crediting the synth and the head model hangs here as a card. The line being said passes through as a ribbon, and the camera follows it back. LEVEL 2: the ribbon flattens into a cue file, a long hall of tick marks, one per word, each with its start and end. Pictures hang off the ticks. At each cue a flash goes off: a frame is shot and looked at. LEVEL 3, the far end of the hall: the fitting room. The same line is said over and over to a line-drawn ear that writes down what it heard. "Kazoo" comes out "clue". "Repost" comes out "report". A sound-editor puppet respells. LEVEL 4, behind the ear: the writers' room. A writer puppet types "This is how the show is made." One door carries the hook's own description of itself: "a guard, not a wall". LEVEL 5, the page on the writer's desk: under the brief is the fact sheet (written by the project manager, checked line by line by an auditor), and under that the commission. The camera goes into the commission's word "recursive". LEVEL 6, inside the word: a desk and a head, opening its mouth to say the sentence. The camera goes for the mouth and bounces off: the head is a circle 42 pixels across, the size the profile picture was judged at. A pink dot on its ring is flicked away. Larry, voice over: "That is the bottom. You cannot go into a profile picture." Button, a very small Larry: "Forty-two pixels."
- **format:** an explainer as one continuous push-in
- **speakers:** none
- **words:** at://did:plc:c2x5i7fneh7xbvpvw2r3omes/app.bsky.feed.post/3n25vm7gzms23
- **notes:** EXPLAINS: what the show is made of (the vocal-tract heads, offline audio, cue files, frames shot at every cue), fitting, who writes, and how this script came to exist. RECURSIVE: levels 4 to 6 are the making of the explanation (the writer, the fact sheet, the commission), and level 6 is the whole thing starting again. ENDS: at the profile picture, because a 42-pixel head has no mouth to go into; Larry says so. THREE DIMENSIONS: every level is a place, and the picture moves by going through a mouth, along a line, through an ear, into a page. Only the four parts the sheet names are drawn in the head. The mishearings are two of the listed harmless ones, word and what was heard, with no source. The pink dot was on one candidate picture and was removed: it is said as that. Puppets in process roles are house puppets, never a model speaking.

**What the commission asks of this script.** It is an explainer of how the show is made that is recursive (it explains the making of the making-of, and so on down) and staged in three dimensions: each level of the recursion is a place the camera travels into. The studio's heads and stage are line-drawn three-dimensional models in a browser, as the fact sheet describes. You write the words, and on every cue a comment saying what the place is and what the camera does; a builder stages it later. The bible's limit of one joke about the show itself does not apply to this script.

**Larry's turn to the audience is required.** In Larry's own words, the way the cast chapter says he talks, he tells the community two things: that they make this show, and that tagging the show's account, `@latecluster.simcluster.tv`, guides where the show looks. The terms' section "The turn to the audience" sets what he may and may not say. In short: the invitation is to the community the show is about and says so at least once; a tag is a look and nothing more; it promises no place, no reply and nothing about what gets used; it never says a tag from anyone at all is read. Make it grow out of the piece, not sit on the end of it.

**An auditor's rulings on these two ideas:**

- Idea 2:25 as written never says who the invitation is for. Larry's turn must make plain, at least once, that it is addressed to the community the show is about.
- Idea 1:25 was written before the owner's addition and has no turn to the audience. Yours must have one.

**Exact text.** Take every post's text from the kept card by its address. Never retype one. A post a house character reads aloud is exact too, with its address in a comment on the line.

**Pronouns.** Refer to a real person by name or handle. Use a pronoun only where `production/research/pronouns.md` states one, and then that one. Where none is stated, use the name, or "they". Never infer one. The ideas above may have got this wrong; do not copy them.

**Words only.** Do not render audio and do not chase what the engine hears: a fitting tool and a sound editor do that, and send back only a line that cannot be said.

**Television.** Nothing on screen for the viewer to read in silence. There is no episode experiment for a commission.
