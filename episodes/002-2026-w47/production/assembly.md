## The order, and why

1. **The three segments with a wordless stretch are never neighbours.** They sit first, fourth and
   sixth, with the stretches at about 1:00, 4:50 and 7:50.
2. **The interview runs before the song, with two segments between them.** The interview tells the
   fact the two share. The song then reads on from the very post the interview stopped in, and its
   button counts one past the interview's number. The other way round, the interview's first
   exchange would arrive already answered.
3. **The pen is first** because it is the clearest thing in the episode to a viewer who has never
   seen the show: one pen, one sheet, 88 replies. It also puts three segments between Sophie's
   meow and the song's cat.
4. **Chess is last** because its button is the strongest last line the episode has, and the close
   grows out of it without looking back at anything.
5. **Top Chicken is third**, the episode's one change of presenter and its loudest segment, after
   two quiet pieces at Larry's desk.
6. **The coffee segment cannot sit beside the interview.** Both seat a guest in the chair beside
   Larry, and one guest is David and the other is Dave.
7. **The song is fifth**, a quiet piece with livelier ones on both sides and not at the close.

**What this order costs.** Both reviewers called the interview's parody line the best single joke
of the drafts, and here it lands at 2:20, in the front half. The bible wants the biggest laugh in
the back half. I could not have both that and a song that is not the closer: the interview has to
come before the song and apart from it, so an interview in the back half makes the song the last
segment. The order that does that is coffee, Top Chicken, plotter, interview, chess, song. I chose
against it because the song is the one segment whose turn depends on a sound nobody has heard yet,
and I did not want the episode to end on its least-tested piece. The back half as ordered holds
what one reviewer called the funniest real exchange in the set (the mug thread) and what the
other called the best button (chess). If a reviewer weighs it the other way, the alternative
order needs these script changes and no others: the title line and its applause move from the
plotter to the coffee segment, chess gets its drafted first line back, and the four lines of the
close come off chess.

## The open and the close

There is no `open` segment and no `close` segment. Each is a few seconds inside the segment it
belongs to, so the rundown is the six selected segments.

**The open** is the first five seconds of segment 1. The pen is over the blank sheet from the first
frame, with its motor's hum. Sibyl says the title and the host over it ("This is The Late Cluster,
with Larry Nx.", 1.5 s to 4.1 s), there is a short round of applause, and Larry's first word, at
5.4 s, is the material ("Saturday. One pen plotter, one sheet of paper, one post."). No greeting,
no joke of the show's own.

**The close** is four lines after the chess button, at the same table, from 8:10:

> SIBYL: The resignation is accepted. / SIBYL: I will say good night. / LARRY: I meant the game. /
> SIBYL: Good night.

Then a sting, long applause and the credits. It is about the resignation and nothing else: it
reviews nothing and names no other segment. It comes from who Sibyl is (she outranks Larry on
procedure and never takes a ruling back).

*For the reviewer to judge:* the interview already spends the episode's one joke about the show.
I read the close as a joke between two house characters about a game, not about the show. If it
reads as a second joke about the show, cut the four lines and the cue `sign-off`: the segment then
ends on "I resign.", its own button, and the close still does its two jobs.

**Joins.** Three lines were written for the joins and nothing else was added between segments.

- Top Chicken: Larry throws to the desk by name ("And now, the sports. Eppy?"), the one change of
  presenter in the episode.
- The song: one line that says whose cat this is ("About the cat voice built by David.").
- Chess: its first line was "Tonight, we play a new game."; as the last segment it is "One more
  game before we go."

No line ties one segment to another, and nothing says or tallies that things were made because
someone asked.
