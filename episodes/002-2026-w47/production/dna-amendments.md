## Experiment

What it was: in each segment drafted from a made thing, one stretch of at least ten seconds with
no spoken or sung word and no crowd sound, in which the thing is shown in use and the segment's
turn happens as something the thing does. It tested the joke chapter's rule that the voice says
the setup and the punch. Its measure: the stretch is there in the cues, and a cold viewer given
only the stretch names the turn the writer named.

Verdict: worked, in the two stretches that aired; partly run.

Why. Three stretches were built. Two aired, at 13.15 s and 11.87 s by the cues; the third was
cut in the owner's trim before air, so by the measure's own words the experiment was partly run.
Three cold viewers, each given one stretch as stills with no sound, named the turn for the two
that aired and not for the one that was cut. The owner, watching cold, kept the same two and cut
the same one. The proxy was weak (stills, no sound, a small model), so what the audience made of
the two is still to be read from the next window's reactions.

What the two that worked had in common is what the amendment says: the turn was a change of
state the viewer could watch happen. In the one that did not, the turn had to be read off the
screen and rested on a fact the viewer was only told afterwards.

## Edits to the DNA in this pull request

- `06-jokes.md`: a thing that is shown working may take the punch itself, with no word said.
  One new point and a constructed illustration under "What the voice does and what the screen
  does". It lands when the turn is something the thing visibly does; not when the turn must be
  read or rests on a fact the viewer has not been told; once in an episode, not in every segment.
- `10-process.md`: rows A16 and B8 and two "good work" sentences now follow the owner's ruling on pronunciation.
  The ruling of 21 November 2026 is that best effort on pronunciation is sufficient because the
  subtitles carry the line. It was already written into the voices chapter and the workflow;
  these four places still said the opposite.

  In row A16 a pass is now: the heard text of the final mix read for every line, nothing heard
  that is crude, offensive, alarming or out of bounds, and every line still heard differently
  about a real person listed for the owner's watch with its exact words on screen. A name heard
  as another word no longer fails by itself.

  In row B8 a score of 1 is now soft punchlines or names that the caption and a picture carry,
  and 0 is a punchline or a name that is lost: not heard and not on screen. Before, a name the
  voice could not say scored 0, and a 0 in this row sends an episode back, which the ruling
  forbids for pronunciation alone.

  Under "Drafting a segment" and "Assembling an episode", "heard as written" became fitted on a
  best effort, with the heard text read and nothing crude, alarming or out of bounds heard.
