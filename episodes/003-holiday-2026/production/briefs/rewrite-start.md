| `q-does-it-help` (said back) | Does it help? |

Every line here that describes the world points one way: things are fast, fair is hard to
find, people are rattled. Nothing says that wonderful things are happening too. The owner's
point is that what unsettles people is how much is happening, the terrible and the wonderful
together and both at speed, and Larry should sound like a man who sees both.

## The task

Write **three different versions** of this stretch for the owner to choose between. Different
in approach, not three wordings of one idea. In each:

- Larry is the same man as in the rest of the script: off the air, dry, slower to joke, a
  reader and never the expert, honest about not having an answer. Read what comes after (the
  cook, the crossing where the viewer asks "What if none of it ever gets put right?", the
  door) and make sure your version still leads into it. The hard question at the crossing
  stays; this stretch should not answer it early or make it land from nowhere.
- He names nothing. No event, person, place, date or side, and nothing that could be read as
  one. The terrible and the wonderful are both left to the viewer's own week. Chapter 9's
  out-of-bounds subjects stay out as references too: no illness, death, disaster, money
  trouble, politics. Plain human words for both halves.
- It is not a balance sheet and not a pep talk. He does not tell the viewer the good outweighs
  the bad, or how to feel. He says what it is like.
- Short lines, common words, in a voice that says long or unusual words badly.
- **About the same length**: the stretch is 50 words now. Stay between 42 and 58, and keep
  the same number of lines or one more or one fewer, because the street under it is staged
  and timed.
- Keep `how-have-you-been`, the silence, and `yeah-me-too` as they are unless your approach
  truly needs one changed, and keep `q-does-it-help` and `some-nights`, which are the first
  use of the viewer's question said back. `no-joke` with `i-looked` is the lightest moment in
  the first minute and both reviewers wanted it kept; keep it if it still follows from your
  new line, and say so if it does not.
- A line that keeps its job keeps its id. A new line gets a new id. Say which ids are
  unchanged, changed and new.
- Nothing new has to be drawn. The street in this stretch is what it is: Larry walking in
  profile, lamps, dark building fronts with a few lit windows, a car passing. If one of your
  versions leans on something he sees, it must be one of those.
