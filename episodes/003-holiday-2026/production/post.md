## The description for the page

Offered in place of the present one ("A holiday special. No show tonight: Larry, off the air,
walks you home through the cold."):

```
A holiday special in place of a usual episode: no guests, no audience, nothing from the week's posts. The studio is switched off, and for four minutes Larry, off the air, walks home through the cold with you beside him.
```

219 characters, two sentences.

Why change it: the present one is a good line for the day it goes out, and less good for the
archive. A year on, "tonight" has no night to point at, and a reader going down the list
cannot tell from it why this entry has no guests and runs half the length of the ones beside
it. The new one says what is absent, how long it is, and where the viewer is (beside him),
and still says nothing of the door, the table or what any of it is for. If the owner would
sooner keep the archive's lines short, the present one breaks no rule and can stay.

## For whoever sends it

- No mention, link, hashtag or emoji in any of them, and the holiday is not named.
- A and B say "tonight" in a post that goes out in the morning; it is Larry's word and the
  premise. C does not use it.
- "Four minutes" is the brief's figure and the script notes' (251 seconds). I have not seen
  the posting cut. If it runs nearer four and a half with its cards, A's last two words, the
  description's "for four minutes" and the alt text's "about four minutes" want a look.
- C's "talk show performed by puppets" is the only place a post text says what the show is.
  Nothing in A or B tells a stranger the heads are puppets or the voices synthesized; the
  video and the alt text do.
