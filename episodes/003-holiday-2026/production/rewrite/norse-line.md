# The Norse line on the last block

For the owner's choice of 27 November 2026 ("The Norse line"). `this-block` is unchanged and
still first. After it come four new lines, 24 spoken words, the quoted nine included:

| id | words | where he looks |

The count is 427 spoken words, from 403, and 438 with the busker's eleven sung syllables.

**How it arrives.** The cook and the couplet are brought in from outside: "There's a story",
"There's an old Tamil book". This one is not brought in. `one-line` corrects the line before
it: he said he walks this block on his own, and that is not quite so, because he has a line he
walks it with. Then he says it, as on any other night, to the road. The source is named before
the words, in the card's wording and without the poem's name, and nothing is announced. "One
line" is also the honest size of what he knows; he makes no claim about the rest of the poem.

**Why it is his.** He says every night's line on the one night it is true, and hears it only
once it is out. That changes the glance: it was a look at company, and now it is a man checking
whether he was heard. It is the only time his head turns on the block.

**The half he takes back.** The earlier draft's words, unchanged, as two beats: the caption no
longer shows "Mostly." before he says it, and for a step or two the retraction is whole. If the
voice will not carry one word alone, they can be one beat again.

**The door.** This moment says he is glad we came and stops. It uses none of the door's words
(guest, outside, table, come up) and leaves "company" for `company` at the table, where he says
the same thing plainly to his friends. He shelters behind an old text twice in thirty seconds:
the first he disowns, the second he pleads. That reads as one man.

**Left for others.** The footsteps under the block (`steps-snow` and its step back) are as they
were, and are now about twelve seconds short. Out of date, and outside what the brief let me
change: the comment at the top of `script.js` (three ideas, one translation), and `builder` and
`seconds` in `notes.json`.
