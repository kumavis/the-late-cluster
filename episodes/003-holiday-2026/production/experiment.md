# The experiment

**What is tried.** Four earnest minutes in Larry's own words, outside the studio, with no
posts, no guests and no audience. The bible keeps sincerity to one short moment in the
community's words; this special is the test of whether the show can hold a sincere piece of
its own without it becoming a speech. The owner set the form (`owner-notes.md`); the two
reviewers at select each proposed how to check it, and their measures are below, unchanged.

**The measure, from reviewer 1.** Show the finished special once, with sound, to three people
who have not read it: it worked if at least two can say back in their own words one thing
Larry offered that a person could do tonight and what happened when he got home, and if none
of them, asked to point at a moment where they were told how to feel, can point at a line of
Larry's other than the owner's closing lines.

**The measure, from reviewer 2.** Play the finished special once, with sound, to three people
who have not read the script, and ask each what Larry told them and which moment stayed with
them: it worked if at least two give the don't-have-to-finish, don't-get-to-quit idea in their
own words, at least two name something that happens and not something they were told to feel,
and none calls it a speech, a lesson or a list; and its cues hold no laugh, applause or sting.

**What can be checked before it airs.** The last clause can be read off the script. The rest
needs people watching with sound, which the show's cold viewers cannot do: they are given
still frames and nothing else. So before the owner's watch the cold-viewer test covers what
frames can show, the stretches that carry meaning without a word: the cook who slows down
while the room hurries, the sign that stays red after Larry has finished, the figure in the
top window before the door opens, and the look, the chair and the bowl at the table. The
people test is the owner's watch and whatever the owner hears back after it airs.
