## Verdict: pass

1. **The quotation.** The caption `"Rich did I feel when a comrade I found".` is **exact**
   against the third line of the card's stanza 47. The card's line ends in a comma before "For
   man is man's delight."; the caption puts the stop outside the closing mark, which says the
   source's sentence does not end there. It is honest. One line is a fair quotation: it is a
   complete clause, and its sense (finding a companion is riches) is the stanza's own, as the
   card tells it. Leaving out the line after it does not change what it means. The card's warning
   (stanza 45, the false friend) is about not claiming the poem is all kindness, and nothing
   here claims that.
2. **The attribution.** "an old Norse poem" is the card's "Easily told as" wording. "One line"
   is about what Larry carries with him, not about the poem, and the card supports it as one
   line. Nothing is said or implied about who speaks in the poem: no Odin, no "High One", no
   Vikings, no named author. "That's the poem talking." puts the words with the poem, where
   the card puts them.
3. **Traps and respect.** Nothing of runes, warriors or symbols in the lines or the cue comments.
   The `comrade` comment asks that no words go on screen. Neither stanzas 76 to 77 (death) nor
   stanza 45 is used. "Comrade" is Bellows's own word, and here it can only mean a companion on
   the road (as in round 1 of the drafts).
4. **The credits.** `episode.json` Quoted: "The Hávamál, stanza 47 / translated by Henry Adams
   Bellows, "The Poetic Edda", 1923", and `notes.json` `credits`: Hávamál, stanza 47, in The
   Poetic Edda, Bellows, 1923. Both are exact to the card (stanza 47 is Bellows's numbering, and
   it is Bellows's translation that is credited). The card records no other edition, so nothing
   is implied about one. Gorfinkle's line keeps "first published 1913", as round 1 asked. Every
   quoted translation is credited (two). "Told from" lists only the two works told (the Zhuangzi
   and Tirukkural chapter 9). Nothing is credited that is neither quoted nor told.
5. **R10 and invention.** None of the four lines names or hints at an event, person, place or
   side, or touches any R10 subject. "Rich" is the poem's figure for companionship, not money.
   Nothing is invented as fact: Larry walking this block with the line is his own fiction about
   himself.
6. **Word count.** **427** spoken words, 438 with the busker's 11 sung syllables. Within 400 to
   470 either way. `notes.json` `"words": 438` is the count with the sung syllables, and that
   is how round 1 recorded the figure.
