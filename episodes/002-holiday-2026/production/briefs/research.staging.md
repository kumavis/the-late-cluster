## The task

Three writers are about to draft a four-minute special that leaves the studio. They write
`cue` beats for everything the picture must do, and they need to know what a cue can ask for.
Write them a short, plain account, two pages at most, of:

1. **What the kit already does** that this special could use: how a set is drawn, whether a
   set can be dimmed or switched off, how the camera moves and cuts, how puppets are built
   (what of the body exists: do they have legs, can one walk, turn, sit, put something on),
   how a costume is defined, how many puppets can be on screen at once, what props and
   lighting exist, what sound beds and effects exist besides the studio audience and the band.
2. **What would be new** for each thing the outline asks for, with a rough size for each
   (small: an hour of a builder's work; medium: an afternoon; large: a day or more) and the
   simplest honest way to do it in the show's vector look:
   - the studio at night with everything switched off, and Larry in it out of costume
   - Larry in a coat for the cold, and putting on a scarf on camera
   - leaving the studio and walking down a city street at night, for about three minutes:
     the walk itself, and the street going by
   - things passed on the way: a lively restaurant seen through its window, a busker, and two
     or three other street scenes you think the look can carry well
   - the viewer as a walking companion who is never seen or heard
   - arriving at Larry's apartment: a long table, a meal, and the whole house cast (Sibyl, Hy,
     Al, Dee, Pete, Eppy) around it, out of their show costumes
   - street sound, a busker's music, the sound of a room full of friends
3. **What to avoid asking for:** anything in the outline, or likely to be written, that the
   studio cannot do well, with the nearest thing it can do instead.
4. **A plain vocabulary for cues:** a dozen or so example cue comments of the kind a writer
   should write for this special, so three drafts ask for comparable things.

Describe; do not design the special or choose its scenes. Give no opinion on what Larry should
say. Where you are not sure whether the kit can do something, say so and say what you would
have to try.
