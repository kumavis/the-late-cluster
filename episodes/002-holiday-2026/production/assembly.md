# Assembly: the final script of the holiday special

The script is `segments/walk/script.js`, with `segments/walk/notes.json`. It starts from
writer-2's draft and answers `select.md`. 408 words by the count command; about 243 seconds by
arithmetic, not a render.

## 1. What it is

- **Title:** The Walk Home. (The base's title was a line that is no longer in it.)
- **Retell:** The one where there's no show, so Larry walks you home through the cold, tells
  you what an old cook and an old rabbi knew about hard parts and unfinished work, and then
  can't leave you standing outside his own door.
- **Feeling:** Rattled, walked home, let in.

## 3. The reasons, answered

- **The first idea is the one a viewer would miss least.** Cut whole, emperor and window. The
  first idea is now the cook, and it starts from something Larry has stopped to look at.
- **The three ideas have one shape.** They now arrive three ways. The cook: he sees one, tells
  the story, the picture lands on the word, and nothing is said after it. Tarfon: we ask, he
  does not know, he quotes, he says where he found it and how he borrows it, and the street
  does not answer. The couplet: not offered as wisdom at all, but as the reason he cannot leave
  us outside, and then the house does it back to him. The busker has no book.
- **`cook-keep`.** Cut. In its place, 2.4 seconds of the cook going slowly while the room
  hurries.
- **`walk-sign`.** The sign stays red for 2.6 seconds after his last line and then changes with
  one tick: no chirp, no look up, no remark. `didnt-arrange` is out, because the line only
  means something if the change is a coincidence, and the coincidence was the applause.
- **The table is a welcome party, not a meal.** They waited ("Nobody has started", every plate
  clean, his chair empty). A chair and a place are made in front of us, a bowl stops at our
  plate, and the meal then runs for three seconds from our chair before Larry turns. Pete
  speaks. Hy speaks and is later mid-conversation with Dee; the bowl comes from Dee and the
  laugh is hers. Of the six, only Al and Sibyl say anything about us, and neither says it to
  us.
- **`house-rule`.** Cut. The welcome is "Chair.", a place sliding in and being served.
  Sibyl's one rule is procedure, and Larry loses an argument about it.
- **Coming in costs one line.** The restaurant puts us outside lit glass. At his building he
  expects they have started without him, turns to go in, and we are still on the pavement. He
  says our question back, cannot answer it straight, and the door is opened from upstairs
  before he can turn.
- **Larry is honest but not personal.** `mail-it` (why he came in), the asked question and
  "Yeah. Me too.", `always-stop`, `used-book` (where he met it), `guest-in-sentence`,
  `this-block` with the look ahead and the glance, `started-without-me`, and "I had company."
- **`q-all-right`.** Gone, replaced by writer-3's question that we answer unheard. Each of the
  three say-backs has its own `listen-` cue, a turn, a held gap of 1.2 to 1.6 seconds, and the
  question as a beat by itself. The first is a light one, so the device is learned before the
  hard one.
- **The tune.** No payoff at the table and nobody hums it. It stays on the street: it stops
  short, starts again behind us, and `never-ends` says the one thing the singing voice may not
  make plain. Both sung beats are uncaptioned.
- **`lights-off`, `mostly-lights`.** Cut. `mail-it` is there instead.
- **Would not be missed.** `full-house`, the steam grate and `house-record` are cut.

None of the five items stopped at the repetition gate is in it in any form.

## 4. What I left out that the reviewers praised

- **The Norse line** (writer-3 `norse` to `poem-talking`): three ideas is the ceiling and the
  door needed its text. Its first line, its look ahead and its glance are kept.
- **`didnt-arrange`** (base): see `walk-sign` above.
- **`cook-keep`** (base; reviewer 2): both agreed the picture lands without it.
- **"There is always one extra chair"** (base; reviewer 2): it says what the evening means.
- **Dee finishing the tune** (base; passed with conditions): nothing at the table would tell a
  viewer it was the street's.
- **`story`** (writer-1; reviewer 2): the restaurant is the cook's, and a quip there made two
  things to look at in one window.
- **`dessert-anyway`** (writer-3; reviewer 2): the same reason.
- **`stretched`** (writer-1; reviewer 1): `guest-in-sentence` does that job, and one is enough.
- **`brought`** (writer-1): "I had company." does it and answers `this-block`.
- **`record`** and writer-3's **`house-rule`**: Sibyl says they waited in four words; a count
  of minutes and a second rule were a third and fourth beat on lateness.
- **The lid** (writer-1 `lid-off`): a lid that lifts itself was marked down. It is off at the
  cut, and a bowl comes along instead.
- **The second say-back at the busker** (base `q-singing-to`): three say-backs, each a hinge,
  wear less than four.

## 5. For the owner

- **The light.** Either it stays red after he has said his piece and changes a few seconds
  later with nobody remarking (as written), or it changes on his last word and he says "I
  didn't arrange that." Both reviewers liked that line and both said its timing made the
  street applaud him.
- **The third idea.** Either the Tamil couplet at his door, as the reason he cannot leave us
  outside (as written), or the Norse line on the last block, "Rich did I feel when a comrade I
  found", then "That's the poem talking. Mostly.", with the door a plain invitation. Not both.
- **The names.** Either Larry says "the Zhuangzi" and "the Tirukkural" aloud (as written), or
  he says "an old Chinese book" and "an old Tamil book" and the credits carry the names. The
  cards allow both, and the voice will find both names hard.
- **"They'll have started without me."** Either he says it at the door and is wrong (as
  written), or it goes, and "Nobody has started." is only news to us.
- **The last laugh before your line.** Either Eppy calls Sibyl's ruling and Dee laughs, four
  seconds before "It's a crazy mixed up world out there" (as written), or the talk ends on
  "It's still the rule." and Eppy has no line.
- **The busker's tune.** Either it stays on the street (as written), or Dee finishes it at the
  table while the room carries on.
- **Length.** About 243 seconds as counted. If it must be under four minutes, the busker's
  second phrase and `never-ends` go first, about six seconds.
