# Select: what the reviewers and the auditor decided

Two reviewers scored the three drafts alone, each 0 to 5 on five questions (does it move, is it
interesting, is it television, is this Larry, do the ideas help), and each ran the repetition
gate with the record of what has aired. One auditor checked every attribution and quotation
against `research/philosophy.md`. Their files are under `review/` and are not for writers. This
file is what crosses: the result, the reasons, and for anything stopped at the repetition gate
one line saying what to steer clear of.

## Result

| draft | reviewer 1 | reviewer 2 | total of 50 | repetition gate | audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| writer-1, "No Show Tonight" | 20 | 19 | 39 | fresh, fresh | pass |
| writer-2, "One Extra Chair" | 22 | 21 | 43 | repeat, repeat | pass |
| writer-3, "The Long Way" | 20 | 22 | 42 | repeat, repeat | pass |

Reviewer 1 picked writer-2 and reviewer 2 picked writer-3, each by a point or two, and each
said their pick was not ready as it stood. On the combined score **writer-2 goes forward as the
base.** Both reviewers, working apart, named the same things the other two drafts do that the
base does not, and those are listed below as open to the final script.

The auditor passed all three: every attribution is in wording its card calls safe and every
quotation matches its card to the word. One instruction from the audit: whatever is quoted,
its translation is named in the credits, not on screen during the walk.

## Stopped at the repetition gate

Each of these is the least a writer needs in order to steer clear. Nothing below may come back
in another form.

1. Larry singing or humming a note that comes out wrong, a band member's dry remark on it, and
   the band's music then putting it right. (In writer-2: `larry-hums`, `hy-flat`, and the way
   `table-tune` answers them; `late` leans on `hy-flat` through its word "also".)
2. A tag in which Larry measures himself against someone who has just made something and comes
   off the lesser. (In writer-2: `waited-for-crowd`.)
3. Larry excusing himself from having an answer by naming his job as host. (In writer-3:
   `no-fix`.)
4. "Doo" as the syllable that stands in for missing words. (In writer-3: `busker-3`.)
5. A closing sung line that brings back an earlier sung line with new words that turn to "you"
   and take the listener in. (In writer-3: `saved-a-seat`. One reviewer passed it and one did
   not; the stricter verdict stands.)

A busker's tune without words, and Dee ending such a tune at the table, were passed by both
reviewers, provided Larry does not sing and the room carries on around her while she does. See
the reasons below before relying on it.

## The reasons sent back with the base (writer-2)

- **The first idea is the one a viewer would miss least** (`windows` to `tomorrow-then`). The
  words are about getting up and the picture is someone going to bed; for about fifteen
  seconds it is a voice over sliding fronts; it answers nothing we asked or saw; it is not
  clear whose "Fine" `tomorrow-then` is; and "One was a Roman emperor" opens it like a list.
  Both reviewers.
- **The three ideas have one shape** (who, the telling, the street answers on cue, Larry's
  button), and by the third a viewer sees it coming. Reviewer 2.
- **`cook-keep`** says in words what `cook-slows` has just shown, and "I keep that one" is a
  summing-up (reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 thought the line would be retold. Both agree the picture
  lands without it.
- **`walk-sign`**: the light changes on the last word of the borrowed line, with a chirp, a
  head tip and a hold. `didnt-arrange` is the right lightness in the right place, but as cued
  the street applauds him: a swell in picture form. Both reviewers.
- **The table is a welcome party, not a meal that was already going.** A lateness gag, a
  ruling and two words. Nothing is served, passed or eaten, Pete never speaks, Hy and Dee have
  nothing to do once the tune is over, and the room of friends is 2.6 seconds of murmur.
  Reviewer 1.
- **`house-rule`**: "There is always one extra chair" states the welcome as a principle, the
  one line in the draft that says what the evening means (reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 counted it
  among the things a viewer would retell, and praised the camera taking the chair.
- **Coming in costs one line** (`this-is-me`). Nothing on the walk has made it a question
  whether we come up, so arriving is a change of place and not something that happens to us.
  Reviewer 1.
- **Larry is honest but not personal**, and the outline asks for personal by name. He tells us
  almost nothing of himself, where he met these books, or what the walk is to him. Reviewer 1.
- **`q-all-right`**: question and answer are one beat with no hold before it, so the first use
  of the device sounds like a figure of speech and not like a question heard. Reviewer 1.
- **The tune**: its payoff asks a viewer to know a wordless seven-note melody again seventy
  seconds later, in the engine's singing voice, with no picture to carry it, and the sung
  beats put a row of "la" on the caption line for about ten seconds. With the stopped lines
  gone, nothing at the table tells a viewer the tune is the street's. Reviewer 1.
- **`lights-off`, `mostly-lights`**: a joke about the show itself, in the place where the
  outline wants him off it. Reviewer 2.
- **Would not be missed**: `full-house` (says what the picture shows); the steam grate at
  `walk-on` (nothing meets it); `house-record` with `dee-laughs` (a second beat on lateness).

## What the other drafts do that the base does not

Named by the reviewers, by draft and line id. These are reasons, and the final script may draw
on them; it need not use any of them.

From **writer-1**:

- `q-come-up`, `tamil-book`, `guest-1`, `guest-2`, `position`, `come-up`, with the cues
  `apartment-door`, `listen-door` and `buzzed-in`: coming in is an event. We are put outside
  lit glass at the restaurant and outside it again at his building, the question is asked, and
  an old text is the reason he cannot leave us on the pavement: the one place in the three
  drafts where an idea is something Larry does and not something he tells. Then the door opens
  from upstairs, because somebody saw him coming. Both reviewers.
- `late`, `brought`, `chair`, `rule`, with the cues `heads-to-us`, `chair-in` and `lid-off`:
  the welcome is done, not stated, and a meal starts while we are there. We learn in four
  words that they waited. Both reviewers.
- `q-how` with the cue `listen-how`, and the cue `the-look`: the question device staged as
  listening. He turns and holds a second as if we had spoken, then says it back; and once he
  answers a look without repeating anything, which keeps the device from wearing. Reviewer 1.
- `used-book`, `stretched`: after the quotation has had its silence, two short lines say where
  he met it and that he has stretched it. Personal, and never the expert. Reviewer 1.
- `story`: says what a table of friends is for, as a remark about strangers in a window, two
  minutes before we reach his. Reviewer 2.

What the reviewers marked down in writer-1, so that it is not carried over with the rest: a
tag on nearly every scene, about one every fifteen seconds, which is the host at work; five
crew greetings in twenty seconds, a roll call; source lines on screen that nobody says; the
Hojoki idea displayed and then declined; `assignment` summing up; a staged gag and a sound
(`glance-back`) between the two halves of the owner's table line, which can read as a joke on
that line; the `buzzer` sound, which may be heard as a wrong answer; a lid that lifts itself.

From **writer-3**:

- `this-block`, `norse`, `norse-road`, `comrade-line`, `poem-talking`, with the cues `comrade`
  and `glance`: he tells the viewer he is glad of the company in a line that is not his, looks
  straight ahead while he says it, glances, and half takes it back. Both reviewers called it
  the most personal and truest moment in the three drafts; the feeling is carried by where his
  head points.
- `how-have-you-been`, the wait, `yeah-me-too`: he asks, we answer in a silence nobody hears,
  and he agrees. The shared unease is set down without either naming it. Both reviewers.
- `mail-it`, `guest`: two lines that are his alone, why he came in for an envelope they would
  have posted, and an acknowledgement that the borrowed sentence belongs to a living tradition.
  Reviewer 1.
- `record`, `counted-in`, `house-rule`, `my-house`, `still-the-rule`, `long-way`: the crew talk
  to him and across him as people who have known him for years, and nobody says so. Nothing on
  the table has been touched, which is how we learn they waited. Both reviewers.
- `dessert-anyway` with the cue `dessert`: says "anyway" with a picture. Reviewer 2.

What the reviewers marked down in writer-3: the spine rests on sung words, which the voice
does least clearly; the quotation is given on one held close-up for about twenty seconds with
the street stopped; the Analects block has two names and two layers of who said what before a
knotty sentence; the last picture is from the doorstep with nothing eaten and Larry never
seated, which a viewer can take as the door closing on them; `that-song` leaves a puzzle open
at the moment the room should be the point; `supposed-to-do` has no cue of its own and can be
heard as Larry thinking aloud; `what-youve-got` asks to hear something and the piece gives us
no way to tell him.

## What the base does that both reviewers want kept

`no-joke` and `i-looked`; the viewer's yes taken in `scarf`; the cook slowing on the word
(`cook-hard-part`, `cook-gently`, the cue `cook-slows`); the crossing, where the viewer asks
the question the special exists for and he says he does not know before he borrows an answer
(`q-put-right`, `dont-know`, `tarfon-1`, `tarfon-2`, `dont-finish`, `dont-quit`,
`didnt-arrange`, with the red of the sign on him); five seconds of snow with no words; the
camera taking a chair at the table; the only text on screen being OPEN, DON'T WALK and WALK.

## For staging, not for the script

- The busker needs a voice of his own, not a house voice.
- The stock title card and end card would put the lit studio before the dark one and after the
  table. That is a change to the studio and is with the project manager.
- The scarf that draws itself on is the owner's stage direction done without hands; the
  staging read's other way, a cut to the collar as it settles, is open to the builder.
