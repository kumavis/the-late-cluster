# Post each episode fresh, not as a reply

A weekly show on Bluesky gets the most surfacing when **each episode goes out as its own top-level post carrying native video, with the episode page linked in the post text**. Running the series as a growing self-thread, with or without a self-repost, is the weaker choice. Bluesky's open-source app code shows why. A reply appended to your own thread does reach followers' Following feeds; the app has an explicit "always show self-threads" rule. But that is about all it reaches. It notifies nobody through the reply mechanism. Bell subscribers who chose "posts" only never hear about it. Feeds built with hosted tools such as SkyFeed drop it, because SkyFeed removes replies by default. Whether Discover and the community "For You" feed accept replies at all is undocumented. The owner's "thread plus repost" idea works mechanically but is not sufficient. The repost shows the reply alone, with no parent or root, and can show the same instalment to followers twice. It does not make the reply eligible for any feed it was not already in. Bluesky itself said in 2024 that self-reposting a thread reply had become unnecessary. Vertical video gets real but narrow special treatment: a full-screen, edge-to-edge presentation in the official app's immersive Video player (only at 9:16 or taller) and a taller tile in phone timelines. No official source and no code shows a ranking boost for vertical video or for video in general. One rule runs through the whole report. The mechanics of rendering, filtering and notifications come from source code and lexicons. Ranking claims for Discover, For You and the Video feed rest mostly on the absence of evidence, because those rankers are closed or undocumented.

## Top-level posts are the only shape every stranger-facing feed accepts

Followers are the reliable audience for any pattern. The surfaces that reach people who do not yet follow the show differ sharply by post shape, and the main question is which of them a reply can enter.

**Discover** is Bluesky's closed-source `whats-hot` feed generator. Bluesky has never published its signals or its rules on replies. The client adds no reply filter of its own: for custom feeds it applies only a content-language filter and muted-thread removal, so whether replies appear is decided entirely server side ([feed-tuners.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/state/preferences/feed-tuners.tsx)). If Discover does return a deep self-reply, the app draws it as root, a "view full thread" gap, the previous instalment and then the new one. The new episode therefore arrives under the show's oldest post ([feed-manip.ts](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/lib/api/feed-manip.ts)). Two official facts do apply. Bluesky's leadership has said repeatedly that it does not demote links: "Unlike other platforms, we don't de-promote your links" ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/bluesky-surpasses-threads-x-referral-traffic-source); [Semafor, Apr 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/04/10/2026/how-bluesky-earned-its-reputation-and-why-it-could-be-the-way-of-the-future)). And the August 2026 setting "hide my posts from algorithmic recommendations" keeps an account out of strangers' Discover, which shows that Discover's normal candidate pool includes non-followed accounts ([Bluesky developer blog, Sep 2026](https://bsky.network/blog/content-visibility-declaration)). The bot account must not turn that setting on. It must also tag each post's language correctly, because the client drops custom-feed posts that are not in the viewer's content languages.

**The community "For You" feed** is run single-handedly by spacecowboy. About **72K people load it daily**, and it is the most-liked custom feed at about 53.8K likes. It uses item-to-item collaborative filtering on likes: "It finds people who liked the same posts as you, and shows you what else they've liked recently" ([atproto.com, Apr 2026](https://atproto.com/blog/serving-the-for-you-feed); [blueskyfeeds.com](https://blueskyfeeds.com/feeds/top)). It ignores the follow graph. A post enters it only by collecting likes from people whose taste overlaps with a viewer's. The playground's default window covers posts **published in the last 24 hours**, with a half-life decay ([foryou.club/playground](https://foryou.club/playground)). Quote posts are clearly candidates, because a diversification option spreads apart "posts that quote the same post". Link and video posts are clearly handled, because dedicated "External links" and "Videos For You" variants exist. No source says whether replies are eligible ([foryou.club](https://foryou.club/)). For a small show, this feed is won or lost in the first day by likes from the existing community.

**Other popular custom feeds** mostly settle the question by construction. SkyFeed, the dominant no-code builder, gives new feeds a "Remove if item is Reply" block by default ([Paul Musgrave guide](https://www.paulmusgrave.info/the-moderately-skilled-persons-guide-to-making-a-bluesky-feed/); [Southern Fried Science](https://www.southernfriedscience.com/a-quick-and-dirty-guide-to-making-custom-feeds-on-bluesky/)). SkyFeed's OnlyPosts (about 21K likes) explicitly drops replies and reposts ([blueskyfeeds.com](https://blueskyfeeds.com/feeds/top)). Feed generators return post URIs from their own firehose index ([atproto feeds guide](https://atproto.com/guides/feeds)), so reposting a reply does nothing to change a feed's verdict on it. One feed suits a weekly account particularly well: Quiet Posters (about 16.7K likes) shows "posts from your quieter follows". For the show's niche community, the feeds that matter are that community's own topic feeds. Each one's rules have to be read from its description or confirmed with its operator.

| Surface | (a) Running self-thread | (b) Self-quote chain | (c) Standalone posts | (d) Thread plus self-repost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | Reply eligibility undocumented; if shown, it appears under the old root | Top-level post; no documented quote penalty | Top-level post; links not demoted (official) | Same as (a); no evidence that self-reposts count |
| For You | Reply eligibility unknown | Eligible (quotes are candidates) | Eligible | Same as (a) |
| SkyFeed-style topic feeds, OnlyPosts | Excluded by default | Usually included | Included | Excluded; repost does not change this |
| Following | Always shown as root, gap, parent, new reply | Shown, except to users who hide quote posts | Shown | Bare repost plus possibly the thread slice |
| Bell subscribers ("posts" only) | Not notified | Notified | Notified | Not notified (inferred) |
| Archive for newcomers | Best: any instalment loads all parents, numbered n/N | Weak: one hop back at a time | Profile Posts and Videos tabs, pinned post | Same as (a), but entry via a context-free repost |

## Every pattern reaches the Following feed, and almost none notifies anyone

In the official app's Following feed, a followed author's self-reply always passes the reply filter. The code comment says "Always show self-threads", and self-threads survive even for users who hide replies ([feed-manip.ts](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/lib/api/feed-manip.ts)). Bluesky's August 2024 change confirmed the behaviour: replying to an older thread of yours "bumps" it into followers' feeds, "without having to repost your own reply to surface it" ([Bluesky blog, Aug 2024](https://bsky.social/about/blog/08-28-2024-anti-toxicity-features); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2844489)). Pattern (a) therefore reaches followers. The cost is presentation. Each week the follower sees episode 1 on top, a gap, last week's episode and then the new one. Since August 2026 a thread-position badge such as "37/37" helps the reader see which post is new ([commit history](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/commits/main/src/screens/PostThread/components/ThreadItemPostNumber.tsx)). A standalone or quote post appears as itself. One exception: a viewer who has switched on "hide quote posts" never sees a quote-chain instalment in Following ([feed-tuners.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/state/preferences/feed-tuners.tsx)). These conclusions come from client code. The server's own timeline ordering was not verified.

Notifications favour top-level posts, though less dramatically than people assume, because Bluesky has no general "notify followers" behaviour. When you append a reply, reply notifications go only to the authors of the five nearest ancestor posts, and the poster is excluded. A self-thread reply therefore notifies no one, including people who liked or replied to earlier episodes. A self-quote notifies no one either, and Bluesky has no thread-subscription feature ([atproto post indexer](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/bsky/src/data-plane/server/indexing/plugins/post.ts); [listNotifications lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/api/lexicons/app/bsky/notification/listNotifications.json)). The one push channel is **activity notifications**, the bell added in July 2025. Subscribers choose "posts" or "posts and replies" ([9to5Mac, Jul 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/09/bluesky-gets-activity-notifications-as-it-tries-to-lure-news-and-sports-users/)), and the subscription record holds exactly those two booleans ([activitySubscription defs](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/api/lexicons/app/bsky/notification/defs.json)). A top-level episode, whether standalone or a self-quote, reaches every bell subscriber. A reply instalment reaches only those who also opted into replies. A self-repost almost certainly reaches none, since subscriptions cover posts and replies only. For a show whose audience is a few hundred to a few thousand people, asking fans to tap the bell is the closest thing to a mailing list. It works only for top-level posts.

## "Thread plus repost" re-shows the reply without context and unlocks nothing new

Pattern (d) is allowed: a repost record can point at any post, including your own reply ([repost lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/api/lexicons/app/bsky/feed/repost.json)). Its effect is narrower than the owner hopes. When a feed item is a repost, the app adds only the reposted post and stops. The code comment reads: "Reposting a reply elevates it to top-level, so its parent/root won't be displayed" ([feed-manip.ts](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/lib/api/feed-manip.ts)). Followers therefore see a bare "Reposted by" post marked as a reply to the same account, which tells a newcomer nothing about the series. The thread-position badge might soften this; that was not verified. Reposts skip the reply filters and thread de-duplication. If the repost slice sorts first, which is likely when the repost follows the reply immediately, de-duplication is deliberately disabled, and the thread slice can still appear below it. **The same follower can see the episode twice**: once without context and once with it. Followers who hide reposts see only the thread slice, so for them (d) is identical to (a).

Beyond Following, the repost changes nothing that can be documented. Feed generators index posts, not the reposter's intent, so a reply that a topic feed excluded stays excluded. No source says Discover or For You counts an author's self-repost as engagement. For You's FAQ does list reposts as an input, but they count as the *reposter's* taste signal ([foryou.club](https://foryou.club/)). The repost is also not a notification to anyone. Pattern (d) does what (a) already did for followers, adds a possible duplicate, and leaves the stranger-facing surfaces exactly where (a) left them. One more caution applies to automating it. The Community Guidelines forbid attempts to "artificially manipulate features or social signals to gain unearned reach" ([Community Guidelines](https://bsky.social/about/support/community-guidelines)). A single self-repost is ordinary behaviour. Repeatedly undoing and redoing reposts to resurface an episode is the kind of pattern that clause describes, and this account is labelled as a bot.

## The thread is the better archive, but profile tabs close most of the gap

Pattern (a) has one real strength: it gives newcomers the easiest path back through earlier episodes. Opening any instalment loads every parent above it. Opening the root loads about six levels below by default, with "read more" controls after that ([getPostThreadV2 lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/api/lexicons/app/bsky/unspecced/getPostThreadV2.json)). The "n/N" numbering labels each instalment. A quote chain is the worst archive. The app renders the quoting post plus one quoted post, hides any quote inside that quote, and leaves the reader to tap back one week at a time ([Embed/index.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/components/Post/Embed/index.tsx)).

Standalone posts recover most of the thread's archive value with tools Bluesky already provides. Every top-level episode lands on the profile's Posts tab. A native-video episode also lands on the **Videos tab** added in January 2025, which becomes an episode grid at no extra cost ([TechCrunch, Jan 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/27/bluesky-adds-video-to-user-profiles)). A pinned post, available since October 2024 ([Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241011-bluesky-1-92-pinned-post)), can point to the site's episode archive. A consistent hashtag, or one of the up to eight hidden `tags` in the post record ([post lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/api/lexicons/app/bsky/feed/post.json)), lets a SkyFeed or Graze feed collect the series. Such a feed works as an index for fans, not as a discovery engine: about 90% of the roughly 158K custom feeds have fewer than ten likes ([blueskyfeeds.com](https://blueskyfeeds.com/)). The one documented serial publisher integration, Transistor's podcast hosting, takes the standalone approach. "Every new episode is automatically posted to Bluesky," and replies to the post sync back as comments on the website ([Transistor changelog, Feb 2025](https://transistor.fm/changelog/bluesky-comments/)).

## Vertical video gets a bigger stage, not a ranking boost

The special treatment for vertical video is real, and every piece of it is about presentation. Bluesky runs an official "Video" feed (`thevids`, described as "Trending videos in the Bluesky network", 7,139 likes). It opens as a full-screen, swipe-up player on iOS and Android, and Discover inserts a "Trending Videos" carousel into it, by default at slice 30, on native apps only ([public API](https://public.api.bsky.app/xrpc/app.bsky.feed.getFeedGenerator?feed=at://did:plc:z72i7hdynmk6r22z27h6tvur/app.bsky.feed.generator/thevids); [PostFeed.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/view/com/posts/PostFeed.tsx)). In that player, only videos with width/height **at or below 9/16** fill the screen. Everything wider, including 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9, is letterboxed on black ([VideoFeed/index.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/screens/VideoFeed/index.tsx)). In phone timelines, a video's height is capped at 14:9 of its width. A 16:9 episode is about 0.56 widths tall, while 4:5 is 1.25 and 9:16 hits the 1.56 cap, about **2.8 times the screen space** of the 16:9 cut. On the web, the cap is square ([VideoEmbed/index.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/components/Post/Embed/VideoEmbed/index.tsx)). Nothing official and nothing in the client says the Video feed or Discover ranks vertical video higher, or excludes landscape video. The Video feed is described only as "trending". The third-party video apps, Skylight (380K users and about 95K MAU in January 2026) and Bluescreen, are vertical-first and can show Bluesky videos. Whether they show landscape Bluesky video well, or at all, is unconfirmed ([TechCrunch, Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/26/tiktok-alternative-skylight-soars-to-380k-users-after-tiktok-u-s-deal-finalized); [TechCrunch, Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/30/beyond-bluesky-these-are-the-apps-building-social-experiences-on-the-at-protocol)).

The choice between native video and a link card is now a real one, because of an August 2026 change. Bluesky raised the video limit to **10 minutes** on 26 August 2026, and the file limit to 300 MB, so a five-minute episode now fits in a single native upload ([TechCrunch, Aug 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/26/bluesky-now-lets-you-upload-10-minute-long-videos/); [constants.ts](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/lib/constants.ts)). A post holds one embed, so it can carry a video or a link card, never both. The text can still carry a clickable link to the episode page as a rich-text facet ([video lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/api/lexicons/app/bsky/embed/video.json)). The only sizeable dataset, Buffer's early-stage March 2026 sample, found median interactions of **5 for video, 4 for images and 3 for links** on Bluesky ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/)). Native video also unlocks the Video feed, the Discover carousel, the profile Videos tab and autoplay in the timeline. A link card unlocks none of these, though it sends more people to the site, and Bluesky says it does not punish links. Because the show's home is a web page, the right answer is native video *plus* a text link, not a choice between them. For a bot posting through the API, three details decide whether this works. The upload must set `aspectRatio`, or clients render the video as a 1:1 box. Captions should be attached as WebVTT tracks or burned in, because timeline autoplay starts muted ([VideoEmbedInnerNative.tsx](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/components/Post/Embed/VideoEmbed/VideoEmbedInner/VideoEmbedInnerNative.tsx)). And video upload requires a verified email on the account ([Bluesky blog, Sep 2024](https://bsky.social/about/blog/09-11-2024-video)).

## Recommendation and the cheapest tests

Post each episode as a **standalone top-level post**. Attach the native 16:9 video with its true `aspectRatio`, VTT captions and the correct `langs` tag. Put a one-line hook and the episode page URL in the text, and add a consistent series hashtag. Pin a post that points to the site's episode archive, and ask the community to tap the bell. Drop the running thread as the primary vehicle. If the owner values continuity, a self-quote chain is the acceptable variant. Through `recordWithMedia`, a quote can still carry the native video ([recordWithMedia lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/packages/api/lexicons/app/bsky/embed/recordWithMedia.json)). It costs the viewers who hide quote posts and leaves a poor archive. Do not commission a redrawn vertical cut yet. The cheaper probe is a **9:16 export that letterboxes the 16:9 stage between a title band and large captions**. That clears the `<= 9/16` threshold for full-screen playback and earns the maximum timeline height without new animation. Whether the bands read well when the player crops tall video on taller phones needs a look on a real device.

The cheapest experiments all fit the show's weekly rhythm, and each needs only a few weeks to show a direction. Bluesky exposes no impression counts, so expect noisy, small-sample signals and compare likes, reposts and referrals rather than reach ([Buffer help](https://buffer.helpscoutdocs.com/article/855-using-bluesky-with-buffer)). First, alternate weeks between native video with a text link and a link card. Compare interaction counts, and compare site visits carrying the `go.bsky.app` referrer, which Bluesky has attached to outbound clicks since March 2025 ([TechCrunch, Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/20/bluesky-makes-it-easier-for-publishers-to-track-referrals/)). Second, settle For You's reply policy for free: run a test reply and a test top-level post through the "Explain" and "Filtered Posts" tools at foryou.club, or ask its operator. Third, list the target community's own feeds, read their rules, and ask each operator whether replies and bot-labelled accounts are included. That one afternoon decides more about pattern (a) than any amount of reading code. Fourth, follow the bot from a second test account and post one thread-plus-repost episode, to see whether the duplicate appears in a real client. Fifth, run the padded 9:16 export for one or two episodes against the 16:9 cut.

## Conclusion

The owner's intuition gets one thing right: on Bluesky, a thread is the native way to *hold* a series, and nothing else gives newcomers such a clean path back through it. But holding a series is a different job from surfacing a new episode. Every surface that reaches strangers either rejects replies by default or keeps its policy unpublished. A self-repost is a client-side presentation trick, not a new route into any feed. The practical split is to let the profile's Posts and Videos tabs, a pinned post and the website do the archiving, and to let each week's episode compete as a fresh top-level post during the 24 hours in which For You and the trending surfaces actually decide.

The larger lesson is about where the uncertainty sits. Everything that can be checked in code (feed filters, notifications, rendering, aspect-ratio thresholds) points the same way. Everything that remains uncertain sits in closed rankers, and each of them can be probed cheaply from the outside. For a show whose audience is one community, the community's own feed operators and bell-tappers are a stronger lever than any guess about Discover. If they confirm they accept replies and the For You test does too, the thread could return as a strong archive, with each new episode also posted on its own at top level.
